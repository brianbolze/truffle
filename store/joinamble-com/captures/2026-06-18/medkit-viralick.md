- 30 kg/m2 or greater (obesity) or
- 27 kg/m2 or greater (overweight) in the presence of at least one weight-related comorbid condition (e.g., hypertension, type 2 diabetes mellitus, or dyslipidemia).

Speak with your healthcare provider to see if semaglutide may be right for you.

It is not known if semaglutide will cause thyroid tumors or a type of thyroid cancer called medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) in humans.

- Do not use semaglutide if you or any family member has ever had a type of thyroid cancer called medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) or if you have an endocrine system condition called Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2).
- Do not use semaglutide if you have had a serious allergic reaction to semaglutide in the past.

- Have or have had problems with your pancreas or kidneys.

## Storage Information

Most solid oral drugs have a longer shelf life when stored at room temperature (68–77°F / 20–25°C) and shielded from UV radiation in airtight containers. The FDA's Shelf-Life Extension Program showed that many solid form medications stayed potent beyond their expiration date.

_COVID-19:_ \[Dose\] TWICE A DAY for 5–7 days

## Oseltamivir 75 mg

_(generic Tamiflu™)_

**Pharmacologic Category:** Antiviral agent; Influenza neuraminidase inhibitor

**FDA-Approved Uses:**

- Treatment of acute, uncomplicated influenza A and B (within 48 hours of symptom onset)
- Prophylaxis of influenza A and B after exposure

**Common Off-Label Uses:**

- Treatment of influenza in higher-risk patients when started more than 48 hours after symptom onset
- Post-exposure prophylaxis in high-risk settings
- Influenza outbreak control in institutional or healthcare settings

**Typical Adult Dosing:**

_Influenza treatment:_ 75 mg by mouth TWICE DAILY for 5 days; begin as soon as possible and within 48 hours of symptom onset

_Influenza prophylaxis after close contact:_ 75 mg by mouth ONCE DAILY for at least 10 days; begin within 48 hours of exposure when possible

_Influenza prophylaxis during community outbreak:_ 75 mg by mouth ONCE DAILY for up to 6 weeks

**Pregnancy:** Category C. Available data do not suggest an increased risk of major birth defects, but data are limited. Consult a medical provider promptly.

**Breastfeeding:** Passes into breastmilk in low amounts; not expected to cause adverse effects in breastfed infants. Consult with a medical provider.

**Notes:** Not a substitute for annual influenza vaccination. Start treatment as soon as possible after flu symptoms begin. Not effective against viruses other than influenza A or B. Dose adjustment may be needed in patients with kidney impairment.

**Common Side Effects:** Nausea, vomiting, headaches, and pain. Serious side effects include confusion, delirium, and hallucinations.

**Contraindications:** Allergy or hypersensitivity to oseltamivir or any component of the formulation.

**Caution:** Rare serious skin and allergic reactions have been reported — stop use and seek care for rash, swelling, or trouble breathing. Neuropsychiatric events (confusion, delirium, hallucinations) have been reported, particularly in pediatric patients. Seek medical attention if symptoms worsen, breathing difficulty develops, chest pain occurs, or fever persists after improvement.

## Appendix A: Pregnancy, Lactation, and Reproductive Potential Labeling

### Purpose

This appendix summarizes how pregnancy, breastfeeding, and reproductive potential information should be interpreted. It is based on the FDA Pregnancy and Lactation Labeling Rule (PLLR) framework.

**Note:** The FDA no longer uses the legacy pregnancy letter categories (A, B, C, D, X) in current prescription drug labeling. These were removed for being overly simplistic. Current labeling uses narrative risk information instead.

### 8.1 Pregnancy

Current labeling may include:

- **Pregnancy Exposure Registry** — Information on available registries for enrollment
- **Risk Summary** — Summary of known risks including human data, animal data, and pharmacologic information
- **Clinical Considerations** — Disease-associated risks, dose adjustments, maternal/fetal adverse reactions, and labor/delivery considerations
- **Data** — Supporting human and/or animal data

### 8.2 Lactation

Current labeling may include:

- **Risk Summary** — Whether the medication is present in human milk, affects the breastfed child, or affects milk production
- **Clinical Considerations** — Recommendations for minimizing exposure or monitoring the infant
- **Data** — Measured drug levels in milk or infant exposure estimates when available

### 8.3 Females and Males of Reproductive Potential

May include information about pregnancy testing requirements, contraception, and infertility when a medication may affect fertility, embryo-fetal development, or reproductive decision-making.

### Legacy Pregnancy Letter Categories

_(Historical reference only — no longer current FDA standard)_

Benefits may warrant use despite risks.DPositive evidence of human fetal risk. Benefits may still warrant use.XAnimal or human studies showed fetal abnormalities. Risks clearly outweigh any benefit.

### Standard Language for Medication Sheets

**Pregnancy:**

- _Current labeling available:_ "Current labeling states \[risk summary\]. Consult with a medical provider before use during pregnancy."
- _Limited human data:_ "Available human data are limited. Use only if potential benefit justifies potential risk. Consult with a medical provider."
- _Avoid at specific stage:_ "Avoid use during \[trimester/week range\] unless specifically advised by a medical provider. Consult with a medical provider."
- _Contraindicated:_ "Use is contraindicated during pregnancy. Do not use if pregnant or planning to become pregnant."

**Breastfeeding:**

- _Unknown presence in milk:_ "It is not known whether this medication is present in human milk or affects the breastfed infant. Consult with a medical provider."
- _Present in milk:_ "This medication is present in human milk. Consider benefits of breastfeeding alongside clinical need and potential infant risk. Consult with a medical provider."
- _Low infant exposure expected:_ "Available data suggest infant exposure through breastmilk is expected to be low. Consult with a medical provider, especially when nursing a newborn or preterm infant."
- _Not recommended:_ "Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for \[duration\] after the last dose."
- _Contraindicated:_ "Use is contraindicated during breastfeeding. Do not breastfeed during treatment."

_This appendix is for medication education and labeling consistency only. It does not provide medical advice, diagnose any condition, recommend treatment, or replace full prescribing information or guidance from a licensed medical provider._
