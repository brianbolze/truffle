Semaglutide is a glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonist used for chronic weight management, along with a reduced calorie diet and increased physical activity, for people with an initial body mass index (BMI) of:

- 30 kg/m2 or greater (obesity) or
- 27 kg/m2 or greater (overweight) in the presence of at least one weight-related comorbid condition (e.g., hypertension, type 2 diabetes mellitus, or dyslipidemia).

Speak with your healthcare provider to see if semaglutide may be right for you.

**Semaglutide may cause serious side effects, including:**

- **Possible thyroid tumors, including cancer.** Tell your healthcare provider if you get a lump or swelling in your neck, hoarseness, trouble swallowing, or shortness of breath. These may be symptoms of thyroid cancer. In studies with rodents, semaglutide caused thyroid tumors, including thyroid cancer. It is not known if semaglutide will cause thyroid tumors or a type of thyroid cancer called medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) in humans.

**Who should not use semaglutide:**

- Do not use semaglutide if you or any family member has ever had a type of thyroid cancer called medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) or if you have an endocrine system condition called Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2).
- Do not use semaglutide if you have had a serious allergic reaction to semaglutide in the past.

**Before starting semaglutide, tell your healthcare provider if you have any other medical conditions, including if you:**

- Have or have had problems with your pancreas or kidneys.
- Have type 2 diabetes and a history of diabetic retinopathy.
- Have or have had depression, suicidal thoughts, or mental health issues.
- Are pregnant or plan to become pregnant. Semaglutide should not be used during pregnancy and there may be potential risks to an unborn baby from exposure to semaglutide during pregnancy. You should stop using semaglutide for at least 2 months before you plan to become pregnant.
- Are breastfeeding or plan to breastfeed. It is not known if semaglutide passes into your breast milk.

**Tell your healthcare provider about all the medicines you take**. This includes prescription medications, over-the-counter medicines, vitamins, and herbal supplements. Semaglutide may affect the way some medicines work, and some medicines may affect the way semaglutide works.

## Your questions, answered.

### Can I share my kit with other family members?

Each prescription is carefully selected based on the individual’s medical history, current health status and any known allergies provided through the clinical intake form. Because all medications carry the potential for allergic reactions or other side effects, medications included in the kit should never be used by anyone other than the person for whom they were prescribed.

The medications in the kit should be treated with the same care and caution as any physician-prescribed medication. It is important to follow all usage instructions provided by the prescribing healthcare professional and to avoid sharing medications with family members or others unless specifically directed by a medical provider.

### What if I have an allergy to a specific medication in the kit?

If you have a known allergy to medication, you can note that during the enrollment process. Based on the medical history and allergy information provided during the enrollment process, one or more medications may not be prescribed in your kit without substitution.

### Is insurance required?

No. Amble offers transparent, flat-rate pricing without insurance and no hidden fees.  Medication, supplies, and shipping are all included.

### What is the shelf life of medications in this kit?

All prescription medications are labeled with an expiration date, which can be found on the prescription included in your kit. While the duration of the expiration date can vary depending on the medication and formulation, most medications, including antibiotics, commonly have a shelf life of around 2 years. Information about the FDA’s Shelf-Life Extension Program may be found within the Guidebook(included within the kit) and at the following link below:

[https://www.fda.gov/emergency-preparedness-and-response/mcm-legal-regulatory-and-policy-framework/expiration-dating-extension](https://www.fda.gov/emergency-preparedness-and-response/mcm-legal-regulatory-and-policy-framework/expiration-dating-extension)

### How do I store my kit?

It is recommended to store medications in air-tight containers in a cool and dry environment. However, storage requirements can vary for different medications, so we recommend consultingwith your healthcare professional regarding proper storage conditions.

## Amoxicillin-Clavulanate 875 mg / 125 mg

_(generic Augmentin™)_

**Pharmacologic Category:** Antibiotic, Penicillin

**FDA-Approved Uses:**

- Lymphadenitis (lymph node inflammation)
- Mastitis (breast tissue inflammation)
- Otitis media (inner ear infection)
- Acute pharyngitis, Group A streptococcus (strep throat)
- Tonsillitis (tonsil inflammation)
- Urinary tract infection (UTI)

**Common Off-Label Uses:**

- Acute bacterial rhinosinusitis (nasal passage & sinus infection)
- Lung abscess
- Empyema
- Pyomyositis (bacterial infection of skeletal muscle)
- Bite wounds (human, dog, cat)
- Dental infections
- Community-acquired pneumonia, mild
- Diabetic foot infection
- COPD, acute exacerbation

**Typical Adult Dosing:**

_Community-acquired pneumonia, mild:_ 875 mg/125 mg (1 tablet) by mouth TWICE DAILY for 5–10 days _Can be used in combination with Azithromycin or Doxycycline_

_Bite wounds — prophylaxis (asymptomatic exposure):_ 1 tablet by mouth TWICE DAILY for 3–5 days

_Bite wounds — treatment (symptomatic infection):_ 1 tablet by mouth TWICE DAILY for 7–14 days

_Urinary tract infection (UTI), uncomplicated:_ 1 tablet by mouth TWICE DAILY for 3–7 days

_Acute bacterial rhinosinusitis:_ 1 tablet by mouth TWICE DAILY for 5–7 days

_Acute pharyngitis (strep throat):_ 1 tablet by mouth TWICE DAILY for 10 days

**Pregnancy:** Category B. Consult with a medical provider.

**Breastfeeding:** Present in breastmilk; considered compatible with breastfeeding at usual recommended doses.

**Notes:** May be taken with or without food; gastrointestinal side effects are reduced when taken with meals.

**Common Side Effects:** Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, and rash.

**Contraindications:** Do not take if known allergy or hypersensitivity to amoxicillin, clavulanic acid, or other beta-lactam antibiotics (penicillins, cephalosporins), or any component of the formulation. Do not take if there is a history of cholestatic jaundice or hepatic dysfunction with amoxicillin-clavulanate therapy. Avoid use in suspected or confirmed mononucleosis.

**Caution:** Significant drug interactions exist and may require dose/frequency adjustment or avoidance. Prolonged use (>28 days) may result in fungal or bacterial superinfection. Please review the full package insert and consult with a medical provider.

## Azithromycin 250 mg

_(generic Zithromax™, Z-Pak™)_

**Pharmacologic Category:** Antibiotic, Macrolide

**FDA-Approved Uses:**

- Community-acquired pneumonia, mild severity
- Acute pharyngitis, Group A streptococcus (strep throat)
- Acute bacterial sinusitis
- Acute otitis media (inner ear infection)
- Acute bacterial exacerbations of COPD
- Uncomplicated skin infections
- Urethritis and cervicitis (Chlamydia trachomatis)

**Common Off-Label Uses:**

- Babesiosis
- Bartonella (cat scratch disease)
- Infectious traveler's diarrhea
- Pertussis (whooping cough)
- Bronchiectasis
- COVID-19

**Typical Adult Dosing:**

_Community-acquired pneumonia / COVID-19 / Pertussis / Bronchitis:_ 500 mg (2 tablets) on Day 1, then 250 mg (1 tablet) daily on Days 2–5 _or_ 500 mg daily for 3 days

_Traveler's diarrhea:_ 500 mg (2 tablets) daily for 3 days

_STI (chlamydia):_ 1,000 mg (4 tablets) one time; repeat after 1 week if needed

_Pharyngitis / Sinusitis / Tonsillitis / Babesiosis / Bartonella:_ 500 mg (2 tablets) on Day 1, then 250 mg (1 tablet) daily on Days 2–5

**Pregnancy:** Category B.

**Breastfeeding:** Present in breastmilk; use caution. Consult with a medical provider.

**Notes:** May be taken with or without food; gastrointestinal side effects are reduced when taken with meals.

**Common Side Effects:** Diarrhea and nausea.

**Contraindications:** Do not take if known allergy or hypersensitivity to Azithromycin (Zithromax™, Z-Pak™), Clarithromycin (Biaxin™), Erythromycin, or any other macrolide. Do not take if there is a history of cholestatic jaundice/hepatic dysfunction associated with prior azithromycin use. Contraindicated with pimozide.

**Caution:** Significant drug interactions exist. May affect the efficacy of blood thinners such as warfarin, increasing bleeding risk. Prolonged use (>28 days) may result in fungal or bacterial superinfection. Please review the full package insert and consult with a medical provider.

## Doxycycline Hyclate 100 mg

**Pharmacologic Category:** Antibiotic, Tetracycline Derivative

**FDA-Approved Uses:**

- Acne vulgaris
- Anthrax (Bacillus anthracis, bioterror)
- Amebiasis (Entamoeba histolytica)
- Sexually transmitted infections (Chlamydia trachomatis)
- Malaria prophylaxis
- Rickettsia infections
- Rocky Mountain spotted fever (Rickettsia rickettsii)
- Q fever (Coxiella burnetii)
- Typhus

**Common Off-Label Uses:**

- Lyme disease (Borrelia bacteria)
- Community-acquired pneumonia
- Acute bacterial rhinosinusitis
- Skin and soft tissue infections due to Staph aureus
- Tularemia / Rabbit fever (bioterror)
- Plague (Yersinia pestis, bioterror)

**Typical Adult Dosing:**

_Anthrax (bioterror), inhalation post-exposure prophylaxis:_ 100 mg (1 capsule) TWICE DAILY for 42–60 days _Some experts favor longer durations (3–4 months). Consult a local expert._

_Lyme disease — prophylaxis (asymptomatic exposure, tick attached >36 hours):_ 200 mg (2 capsules) ONCE

_Lyme disease — treatment (symptomatic):_ 100 mg (1 capsule) TWICE DAILY for 10–28 days depending on presentation:

- Erythema migrans: 10 days
- Acute neurological disease: 14–21 days
- Carditis: 14–21 days
- Arthritis with neurological involvement: 28 days

_Rocky Mountain Spotted Fever:_ 100 mg TWICE DAILY for 5–7 days, or at least 3 days after fever subsides — whichever is longer

_Q fever, acute symptomatic:_ 100 mg TWICE DAILY for 14 days

_Bite wound — prophylaxis (asymptomatic):_ 100 mg TWICE DAILY for 3–5 days

_Bite wound — treatment (symptomatic):_ 100 mg TWICE DAILY for up to 14 days

_Plague / Tularemia (bioterror):_ 100 mg TWICE DAILY for 7 days _Consult a local expert._

_STI (chlamydia):_ 100 mg TWICE DAILY for 7 days

**Pregnancy:** Category D. Many guidelines consider doxycycline a relative contraindication in pregnant women. Consult with a medical provider.

**Breastfeeding:** Present in breastmilk; use caution. Consult with a medical provider.

**Notes:** Most effective when taken on an empty stomach. May be taken with a meal if stomach upset is a concern, though absorption may be slightly reduced.

**Common Side Effects:** Diarrhea and nausea.

**Contraindications:** Do not take if known allergy or hypersensitivity to doxycycline, other tetracyclines, or any component of the formulation. Do not take concurrently with isotretinoin.

**Caution:** Significant drug interactions exist. May exacerbate myasthenia gravis. Prolonged use (>28 days) may result in fungal or bacterial superinfection. Please review the full package insert and consult with a medical provider.

**Common Side Effects:** Gastrointestinal discomfort, nausea, diarrhea, constipation, headache, dizziness, elevated liver enzymes, rash.

**Contraindications:** Do not take if known allergy or hypersensitivity to diclofenac, aspirin, other NSAIDs, or any component of the formulation. Do not take if history of asthma, urticaria, or allergic reactions after taking aspirin or other NSAIDs. Do not use in the setting of coronary artery bypass graft (CABG) surgery.

**Caution:** NSAIDs may increase the risk of serious cardiovascular thrombotic events, including heart attack and stroke. NSAIDs may increase the risk of serious gastrointestinal events including bleeding, ulceration, and perforation. Use with caution in cardiovascular disease, hypertension, GI bleeding/ulcer history, kidney disease, liver disease, asthma, or bleeding disorders. Avoid taking with other NSAIDs unless directed by a medical provider.

## Albuterol Sulfate

_(generic ProAir™, Proventil™, Ventolin™)_

**Pharmacologic Category:** Short-acting beta-2 adrenergic agonist; bronchodilator

**FDA-Approved Uses:**

- Asthma

**Common Off-Label Uses:**

- Shortness of breath
- Wheezing
- Cough

**Typical Adult Dosing:**

_Asthma exacerbation, mild-to-moderate:_ 2–4 inhalations every 20 minutes for 3 doses. If good response, extend interval to every 3–4 hours as needed. If incomplete response, adjust interval to every 1–3 hours as needed.

_Asthma exacerbation (as needed):_ 1–2 inhalations every 4–6 hours as needed.

_COPD exacerbation:_ 1–2 inhalations every 1 hour for 2–3 doses, then every 2–4 hours as needed.

_Patients with worsening symptoms after initial treatment should seek immediate medical attention._

**Pregnancy:** Category C.

**Breastfeeding:** Considered compatible with breastfeeding.

**Notes:** Albuterol is a rescue medication for rapid relief of bronchospasm (wheezing, chest tightness, shortness of breath, cough). Increasing use or needing more frequent doses may indicate worsening disease and should be evaluated by a medical provider. Prime inhaler before first use or after extended non-use. Albuterol does not replace controller medications such as inhaled corticosteroids when prescribed.

**Common Side Effects:** Excitement, nervousness, tremor, bronchospasm, asthma exacerbation, pharyngitis, rhinitis, upper respiratory tract infection.

**Contraindications:** Allergy or hypersensitivity to albuterol or any component of the formulation. Severe hypersensitivity to milk proteins (dry powder inhalers only).

**Caution:** May cause paradoxical bronchospasm, which can be life-threatening — discontinue and seek medical attention if breathing worsens after use. Use with caution in heart disease, arrhythmias, hypertension, hyperthyroidism, diabetes, seizure disorder, or low potassium. Overuse may increase the risk of serious adverse effects and may signal worsening respiratory disease.

## Potassium Iodide

_(generic iOSAT™ / ThyroSafe™)_

**Pharmacologic Category:** Thyroid blocking agent; radiation medical countermeasure

**FDA-Approved Uses:**

- Helps prevent radioactive iodine from entering the thyroid gland during a nuclear radiation emergency
- Use along with other emergency measures recommended by public officials

**Common Off-Label Uses:** Use is generally limited to public health, radiation emergency, or toxicologic guidance. Not for routine "radiation protection" or for radioactive materials other than radioactive iodine.

**Typical Adult Dosing:**

_Thyroid block after nuclear radiation emergency:_ 1 tablet by mouth ONCE DAILY; continue for 10–14 days or until directed by public health officials or risk of exposure has passed.

**Pregnancy:** Category D. Approved for use in nuclear radiation emergencies. Pregnant patients are at higher risk of thyroid dysfunction and thyroid cancer and should be prioritized for treatment.

**Breastfeeding:** Present in breast milk. Approved for use in nuclear radiation emergencies. Breastfeeding patients should be prioritized for treatment.

**Notes:** Protects only the thyroid gland from radioactive iodine — does not protect the rest of the body or against other radioactive materials. Most effective when taken within 24 hours before or within 4 hours after radioactive iodine exposure. Do not take unless instructed by public health officials or a medical provider. Does not reverse existing thyroid damage and may not provide complete protection.

**Common Side Effects:** Heart rate irregularity, numbness and tingling, confusion, fatigue, fever, acne, rash, hives, goiter, diarrhea, metallic taste, nausea, stomach pain, vomiting, joint pain, weakness, shortness of breath, and wheezing.

**Contraindications:** Allergy or hypersensitivity to iodide or iodine. History of dermatitis herpetiformis, certain types of vasculitis, or nodular thyroid condition with heart disease.

**Caution:** Use with caution in known thyroid disease — chronic use can lead to hypothyroidism. Speak with a medical provider if taking potassium-sparing diuretics (spironolactone, eplerenone).
