## What is Human Growth Hormone?

HGH is an essential hormone. That means that like all hormones, it is one of your body’s chemical messengers. Hormones are produced and secreted by the glands of your [endocrine system.](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/21201-endocrine-system) HGH is produced and secreted by the pituitary gland, a tiny gland at the base of the brain with a very big role. The primary function of the pituitary gland is the production and release of growth hormone.

HGH is the cornerstone of all of the processes that take place that allow growth in children. While HGH is required to grow from a child into a healthy adult, you never “grow” out of your need for HGH. Even when you are fully grown HGH controls cell reproduction. In adults HGH is also vital for bone density, maintaining lean body mass, as well as playing a role in digestion, metabolism and other bodily processes.

However, as vital as HGH is, as you age, the less HGH you produce. As growth hormone levels decrease, your body’s ability to replace and repair cells also decreases, and you experience many of the symptoms we think of as “aging.” This is why many think of [HGH replacement therapy](https://www.kingsbergmedical.com/how-does-growth-hormone-therapy-work/) as a Fountain of Youth because it can slow or even reverse many of the processes of aging.

## What Does Growth Hormone Do?

As you might deduce from its name, HGH is responsible for “growth.” It is HGH that stimulates all of the processes from cellular division to bone growth, which allows children to grow into teens, and teens to achieve their full strength and stature as adults.

While HGH is vitally important during those growing years, we still need HGH even as adults, so that we can continue to replace damaged cells, and to maintain good muscle mass and bone health throughout our lives. And yet, despite the continued need for HGH, the production of growth hormone decreases the older you get. Not surprisingly, your [HGH levels](https://www.kingsbergmedical.com/what-is-the-normal-growth-hormone-level-for-adults/) drop most dramatically as you enter middle age, and all the aches, pains, and other symptoms of “growing old,” begin.

## Age-Related HGH Decline

It has been known for decades that HGH levels drop as you age. As HGH declines it becomes much harder to build muscle and burn fat, and this is why you gain weight more easily the older you get, even if you do not eat more, or exercise less!

It is a medical fact that your ability to produce growth hormone lessens over time. That decline, which is anywhere from 2 percent to 4 percent per year can lead to "adult-onset" or "age-related" growth hormone deficiency. Adults with growth hormone deficiency will experience weakness, increased obesity, loss of energy, sleep disorders, weaker bones, smaller muscles, and sexual issues – pretty much all the downsides and assorted health conditions we relate to “getting old.”

> Even when you are fully grown HGH controls the growth and replacement of your cells. However, as you age, HGH levels decline leading to decreased mental and physcial performance.

As you might imagine, your rate of growth hormone production is highest around the time of puberty. Once you reach adolescence, HGH starts a steady and increasing decline. This age-related decrease in HGH production is well-documented by medical science, but its exact trigger remains unknown. However, many researchers believe that it is due to an increased secretion of [somatostatin](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/22856-somatostatin), which is a hormone that inhibits the production of HGH.

HGH injections are approved for the treatment of GHD in children and adults. As such HGH is generally regarded as safe, but like any prescription medication it is not without potential side effects. Some of the most common side effects fo HGH include the following:

- Muscle pain

- Carpal tunnel syndrome

- Fluid retention

- Breast enlargement

- Injection site reactions


Bear in mind that the above list is only potential side effects. Under normal conditions, most patients go through their treatment with growth hormone with few if any complications. For the majority of individuals in need of them, the benefits of growth hormone shots far outweigh any possible risks. If you are having any issues with your HGH injections, contact your healthcare provider, usually a simple change in dosage or brand of HGH is all that is needed to fix the problem.
