## Fulfillment
- **Pharmacy:** ownership **not stated** — every fulfillment claim on the captured pages names a *network/partner* or *the patient's own retail pharmacy*, never an owned or named LifeMD pharmacy. Boilerplate (footer, /psychiatry + /cardiovascular-health): *"By leveraging its proprietary technology platform, 50-state medical group, and **nationwide mail-order pharmacy network**, LifeMD is increasing access…"*; /how-it-works: *"our nationwide network can bring your medications right to your doorstep"*; /weight-management ("Fill your prescription"): *"Your provider will send your prescription to your **local pharmacy**. If your pharmacy doesn't have your medication, we can explore other options that can be mailed directly to your door."* Lane — the **one** lane statement on the pages is a partner, not an owned facility: /weight-management ISI, *"a provider may prescribe compounded medication, which is prepared by a **state-licensed sterile compounding pharmacy partner**"* (sterile ⇒ a 503B-type lane, but explicitly a *partner*). No named pharmacy entity and no owned-pharmacy sibling domain appear — so the coarse posture is `unclear` (a partner/network/retail mix, none claiming ownership), not `integrated`. (The profile's Strategic read infers "own-pharmacy fulfillment," but that is a consumer-side judgment the pages don't support — they claim only a network/partner.) The affiliated **medical** groups *are* named per specialty — e.g. *"Cardiovascular medical treatment is provided by Deo Medical, P.C. licensed providers"* (/cardiovascular-health) — but those are P.C.s, not pharmacies.

## Credibility & access
- **Health-merchant credibility:** no **LegitScript** seal on the captured pages (BBB "Accredited Business" badge in the footer instead); named clinicians yes — a /medical-team page plus specialty clinical leads surfaced by name (Dr. Tara Scott, Dr. Doug Lucas, Lauren Cramer PA-C for Women's Health; Dr. Rahul Deo, Dr. Calum MacRae for cardiology; Dr. Anthony Puopolo, Dr. Sehgal, Dr. Culpepper for primary/mental health); no PCAB/ACHC/NABP pharmacy accreditation shown (consistent with no owned pharmacy claimed).
- **Controlled-substance Rx:** **non-scheduled only** — stated verbatim (/psychiatry footer): *"Controlled substances, including amphetamines (such as Adderall) or benzodiazepines (such as Xanax and Valium) are not available through LifeMD."* No TRT/testosterone SKU appears in the captured catalog (no /mens hub; the hormone work is women's HRT). Profile likewise: "No controlled substances prescribed."
- **Labs:** **optional** (provider-ordered, not a gating step) — *"If your doctor believes that a lab test would be beneficial… they can order a lab test"* (/how-it-works); program-ordered labs are **at no cost** at **Quest Diagnostics or Labcorp**, else insurance/self-pay. Draw model: **at-home nurse draw OR partner lab** — *"we can send a nurse directly to your house… Or… Quest Diagnostics, LabCorp, or any other convenient facility."* State carve-out: LifeMD cannot cover lab cost for patients in NY/NJ/RI.
- **Payment & commitment:** touches a **payer rail** — *"Insurance Coverage Eligible,"* *"$19 + copay/visit,"* *"Medicare accepted,"* and an *"insurance benefits check service"* for brand-name meds; also **HSA & FSA eligible** (homepage Wegovy module) and **cash-pay options** (*"discounted cash-pay pricing,"* *"Insurance and cash pay options"* /cardiovascular-health). Commitment: **cancel-anytime** — *"$19 at the beginning of each billing cycle… automatically renew unless you choose to cancel… cancel your subscription at any time"* (/membership). Insurance is supported but not required: *"Does this replace insurance? No."*

## Notes
- **anchor_category:** recorded `multi/none` — the front door is a **generalist** ("The Doctor Will See You Now") over a **co-equal specialty grid** (hero cards + "Specialty Care" menu in the same order: Weight Loss · Women's Health · Mental Health · Cardiovascular · Urgent + Primary Care), which is exactly the contract's `multi/none` case ("a generalist leading a co-equal grid"). The single strongest **commercial wedge** is GLP-1 weight loss: it owns the homepage's only dollar-hero (*"Access FDA-approved Wegovy®, starting at just $149‡"*), the first specialty nav slot, and the widest SKU lineup — if a reader wants the lead *vertical* rather than the lead *identity*, it is GLP-1. **Point-in-time snapshot, not fixed:** offerings.md documents live A/B price flicker (Wegovy Pen "$199" ↔ "$499" in one capture) and the site loads PostHog (analytics + session replay + surveys); treat the front-door read as a snapshot.

HSA/FSA eligibility and discounted cash-pay are *also* offered (both noted above); they ride under `bills insurance`, which is the load-bearing distinction.
