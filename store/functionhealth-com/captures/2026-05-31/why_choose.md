#### Biomarkers

[Biomarkers **Lipoprotein (a)** \\
\\
Lipoprotein(a) is a genetically determined, "sticky type" of LDL cholesterol that independently increases the risk of major cardiovascular events like heart attack and stroke, because they are more likely to form plaque in arteries. It's an invaluable test used by cardiologists, especially when there is a family history of heart disease, but is not yet widely adopted in primary care.](https://www.functionhealth.com/biomarkers/lipoprotein-a)

[Biomarkers **Magnesium** \\
\\
Magnesium is an essential mineral involved in hundreds of processes in the body, including building strong bones, maintaining a steady heart rhythm, supporting muscle function, and producing energy. Nearly half of U.S. adults don't get enough magnesium from food, making low levels relatively common. Deficiency can disrupt calcium and potassium balance and may lead to muscle cramps, weakness, or heart problems.](https://www.functionhealth.com/biomarkers/magnesium)

[Biomarkers **Prolactin** \\
\\
Prolactin in men plays a supportive role in reproductive and metabolic health, influencing testosterone balance, sexual function, and even mood regulation. When prolactin levels are too high or too low, it can contribute to problems such as infertility, erectile dysfunction, or indicate pituitary dysfunction.](https://www.functionhealth.com/biomarkers/prolactin-male)

[Biomarkers **DHEA-Sulfate** \\
\\
DHEA-Sulfate (DHEA-S) is one of the most abundant hormones in women and serves as a building block for other hormones, including estrogen and testosterone. Measuring DHEA-S can provide insight into reproductive function, adrenal gland health, and overall hormone balance. Levels that are too high or too low may prompt further testing, as DHEA-S has also been linked with aspects of immune health, longevity, and healthy aging.](https://www.functionhealth.com/biomarkers/dhea-sulfate-female)

[Biomarkers **LDL Small** \\
\\
LDL particles can be small, medium, or large. Smaller, denser particles are considered more harmful because they more easily enter artery walls, promote inflammation, and raise the risk of plaque buildup and cardiovascular disease. Larger particles are less likely to do this and are generally considered less harmful.](https://www.functionhealth.com/biomarkers/ldl-small)
