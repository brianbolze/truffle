#### Biomarkers

[Biomarkers **Alkaline Phosphatase (ALP)** \\
\\
Alkaline phosphatase (ALP) is an enzyme found in the liver, bones, and other tissues that helps break down proteins and support growth. Abnormal ALP levels can point to conditions affecting the liver, bones, or nutrition status. ALP is often interpreted alongside other liver tests (such as AST, ALT, and bilirubin) to give a clearer picture of liver and bone health.](https://www.functionhealth.com/biomarkers/alkaline-phosphatase)

[Biomarkers **HDL Cholesterol** \\
\\
Think of the H in HDL as healthy, helpful, heart-supporting cholesterol. HDL's primary job is to remove excess cholesterol from the blood and transport it to the liver to be eliminated, reducing the risk of cardiovascular disease.](https://www.functionhealth.com/biomarkers/hdl-cholesterol)

[Biomarkers **Testosterone, Total** \\
\\
Testosterone is a sex hormone that supports energy, mood, sex drive, bone and muscle strength, and overall hormone balance. Total testosterone measures all circulating testosterone in the blood, while free testosterone reflects the smaller, active portion available for the body to use. Looking at both provides a clearer picture of hormone health and can help explain concerns such as low energy, changes in mood or libido, or conditions like PCOS.](https://www.functionhealth.com/biomarkers/testosterone-total-female)

[Biomarkers **Aspartate Aminotransferase (AST)** \\
\\
Aspartate aminotransferase (AST) is an enzyme found throughout the body, especially in the liver, heart, and muscles. When these tissues are stressed or damaged, AST can be released into the bloodstream, so higher levels in blood tests may reflect liver, muscle, or heart issues. Because AST can rise for different reasons, it is usually measured alongside other liver enzymes to give a clearer picture of liver health.](https://www.functionhealth.com/biomarkers/aspartate-aminotransferase)

[Biomarkers **Triiodothyronine (T3) Free** \\
\\
Triiodothyronine (T3) Free is the active thyroid hormone that directly regulates metabolism, energy levels, and body temperature. Most T3 is made by converting T4 into this active form. Measuring free T3 helps show how well this conversion is working and how much active thyroid hormone is available for the body to use.](https://www.functionhealth.com/biomarkers/triiodothyronine-t3-free)
