### How it works

Semaglutide injections are administered subcutaneously once per week. By acting on GLP‑1 hormone pathways, semaglutide may help support appetite regulation and influence how the body manages blood sugar. Responses vary among individuals, and treatment should always be used under the guidance of a licensed provider.


### How to take it

Semaglutide is typically taken as a once‑weekly subcutaneous injection in areas such as the abdomen, thigh, or upper arm. Rotate injection sites as instructed by your provider. Your licensed healthcare professional will guide dosing, timing, and administration to help ensure safe use.


### What are the results?

Experiences with semaglutide vary among individuals. Some people report changes in appetite, eating patterns, or weight over time when semaglutide is used alongside healthy nutrition, physical activity, and ongoing provider oversight. Outcomes depend on many factors, including medical history, consistency, and lifestyle habits. Your provider will evaluate your progress and adjust your plan as needed.


### Common Side Effects

- Nausea
- Diarrhea
- Vomiting
- Constipation
- Stomach pain
- Decreased appetite

Some individuals find that these effects are mild and improve as their body adjusts to treatment, while others may experience more persistent discomfort. This list does not include all possible reactions. More serious side effects—such as severe abdominal pain or signs of an allergic reaction—require prompt medical attention. Always speak with your healthcare provider about any symptoms you experience.


### Is it safe?

All medications, including semaglutide, carry potential risks and side effects. People may respond differently based on their medical history and overall health. It is important to use semaglutide only under the care of a licensed healthcare professional who can review potential risks, discuss expected benefits, and monitor your response throughout treatment. Seek medical guidance promptly if you have concerns about how you are responding to the medication.
