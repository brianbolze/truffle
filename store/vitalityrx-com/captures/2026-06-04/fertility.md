## FAQ

How does testosterone affect male fertility and why can testosterone replacement therapy harm fertility?

Testosterone plays a complex role in male fertility. While adequate testosterone levels are essential for sperm production and sexual function, artificially supplementing testosterone through TRT can paradoxically harm fertility. This happens because external testosterone causes the body to shut down its natural production in the testicles, where high concentrations are needed for sperm production. When the pituitary gland detects high blood testosterone from supplements, it reduces the production of FSH and LH, which are crucial hormones for sperm production.

What hormones are most important for male fertility and how do they work together?

Three key hormones work in concert to maintain male fertility: FSH, LH, and testosterone. FSH stimulates sperm production and maturation in the testicles, while LH triggers the production of testosterone by testicular cells. Testosterone then works alongside FSH to support sperm production and maintains sexual function, including libido and erectile function. This delicate hormonal balance is controlled by the pituitary gland, and a deficiency in any of these hormones can significantly impact both fertility and sexual performance.

How does Enclomiphene affect sperm count?

In addition to increasing testosterone levels, enclomiphene has also been shown to increase sperm count. In one 2013 study, enclomiphene citrate elevated sperm counts in 100% of men at three months and the six-month mark. And in a three-month study comparing enclomiphene citrate to topical testosterone, researchers also found that while both testosterone treatments increased testosterone, those taking topical testosterone experienced a significant decline in sperm concentration. This decline rendered more than half (54%) of men in the study to become oligospermic (infertile).
