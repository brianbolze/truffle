Our StoryOur quest for longer lives started with a fight

In 2019, Dr. Elana Miller was diagnosed with lymphoma for the second time. She and her husband, Healthspan founder Daniel Tawfik, began their search for the best physicians and treatments in the field.

After a stem cell transplant, Elana and Daniel sought longer term care with experts in advanced metabolic oncology and mTOR inhibition research. However, these specialists were rarely available and cost a fortune. Fees for one renowned longevity doctor were more than $120,000 per year.

With their scientific and clinical backgrounds, Elana and Daniel knew that drugs weren't driving the cost, but rather the doctors's rare expertise. Daniel began looking for ways to identify and share new developments in longevity science so that anyone could benefit from the research, not just the super wealthy. And as he did, Healthspan was born.

Today, Elana and the business she helped inspire are thriving, as are more than 12,000 Healthspan patients across the country.

#### Effectiveness is everything

All the geroprotective molecules we use show strong mechanistic and clinical support for modulating the nutrient-sensing, inflammatory, and mitochondrial pathways that drive biological aging.

### Dr. Rick Cohen

Dr. Rick Cohen, M.D., is a functional medicine physician and a leading figure in nutrition, sports performance, and longevity medicine. With over 20 years of experience, he has pioneered health and performance protocols focusing on holistic approaches and lifestyle interventions alongside medication protocols. Dr. Cohen's academic credentials include degrees from Duke University and Hahnemann Medical College (Drexel University). He is also affiliated with the American College for the Advancement of Medicine and the American Academy of Anti-Aging Medicine.

![Dr. Elana Miller](https://methodical-vitality-96814f361f.media.strapiapp.com/elana_miller_e685689051.jpg)

### Dr. Elana Miller

Dr. Elana Miller is a two-time lymphoma survivor who has undergone a stem cell transplant. Her experience with rapamycin as part of her treatment led her to research the effects of molecules like it, along with lifestyle interventions, in preventing age-related chronic diseases such as cancer. Dr. Miller's academic credentials include degrees from Harvard and USC Medical School, as well as a residency at UCLA.
