## Overview

EVOLOH manufactures electrolyzer stacks for clean and low-carbon hydrogen production. Its site frames the company around high-throughput manufacturing of electrochemical stacks, local supply chains, commodity materials, and a stack-only model for EPCs, project developers, and industrial hydrogen projects. The captured pages say EVOLOH was founded in 2020 in California, is headquartered in Santa Clara, CA, and has a Manufacturing Center of Excellence in Lowell, MA.

The core product is Nautilus, described as a multi-MW advanced liquid alkaline / anion exchange membrane stack platform intended to reduce balance-of-plant complexity and project execution risk.

## What they offer

- **Nautilus electrolyzer stacks and modules:** Advanced liquid alkaline stack platform for large-scale hydrogen projects; product page says "Up to 50MW per module," and the brochure names "Nautilus 90 (0.5 MW stack)" and "Nautilus 2100 (12 MW module)" with "Pricing below $250/kW for 2026-27 deliveries" `[published]`.
- **S440 packaged hydrogen system:** A 2.5 MW system built around EVOLOH's Nautilus stack for the 3M facility pilot; the project page says it will be operational in 2027 subject to required regulatory approvals, but product pricing is not published `[on-request]`.

## How it works / model

EVOLOH sells stacks, not full electrolyzer plants. The homepage says EPCs can use Nautilus to standardize plant design and simplify engineering, commissioning, and integration with the rest of the plant; it also says new power electronics or heat/gas/fluid management units are not required because integrators can use standard balance-of-plant components. Manufacturing is positioned around high-speed roll-to-roll processes, advanced robotics, real-time optimization tools, and local/domestic supply chains.

The business model appears to be project/equipment sales to industrial buyers and developers. The captured proof pages include a non-refundable-deposit-backed 0.5 GW supply agreement and a commercial-scale pilot project with 3M, but standard contract terms and quote mechanics are not published.

## Positioning & audience

EVOLOH targets EPCs, renewable-energy developers, industrial hydrogen users, and large-scale/off-grid hydrogen projects. Its claimed edge is manufacturing throughput and stack economics rather than a full-project ownership model: the homepage says "We sell only stacks," and the product page emphasizes low-risk technology, compact/lightweight integration, no PGMs or PFSAs, plug-and-play design, electrochemical pressurization, no corrosive electrolytes, and fast load-following response.

The company positions hydrogen scale-up as a manufacturing and supply-chain bottleneck. Its homepage claims "4x" lower factory cost versus competitors' future manufacturing plants, "100%" domestic supply chains, and "10x" lower stack cost versus competitors' future projections.

## Visual & brand impression

Industrial, technical, and unusually restrained for climate hardware. The site uses a deep navy base with cyan/aqua accents, pale gray technical sections, monospaced micro-labels, and clean line/render illustrations of stack manufacturing and electrolyzer modules. The brand reads as engineering-infrastructure first: little lifestyle imagery, strong emphasis on diagrams, numbers, and manufacturing mechanics.

The measured wordmark is a navy EVOLOH wordmark with a cyan circular logomark. The square favicon/logomark is usable at 256px, but it has an opaque white background, so it is not a transparent slide-ready icon on dark backgrounds.

## Strategic read

EVOLOH's site is deliberately narrow: it is not selling a complete hydrogen plant, a financing product, or a developer/operator story. It is trying to own the stack bottleneck. The repeated "we sell only stacks" framing makes the strategic claim simple: standardize the expensive, supply-constrained core module so EPCs and developers can use familiar balance-of-plant components and reduce project delay/cost risk.
