### Intracellular NAD+

**What It Measures**

The level of Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide (NAD+) within your cells.

**Why It Matters**

NAD+ is essential for energy production, DNA repair, and overall cellular function. Declining levels are associated with aging and decreased metabolic efficiency.

### Senescence-Associated β-Galactosidase (SABG or β-gal)

**What It Measures**

Activity levels of β-galactosidase, an enzyme linked to cellular aging.

**Why It Matters**

Elevated SABG indicates the accumulation of senescent cells, which contribute to aging and age-related diseases.

### Reactive Oxygen Metabolite (ROM)

**What It Measures**

Levels of reactive oxygen species (ROS) in the body.

**Why It Matters**

High ROS levels can damage cells, proteins, and DNA, leading to chronic diseases and accelerated aging.

### Total Antioxidant Capacity (TAC)

**What It Measures**

The body’s overall ability to neutralize free radicals.

**Why It Matters**

Adequate TAC protects against oxidative stress, maintaining cellular integrity and reducing disease risk.

### Klotho

**What It Measures**

Levels of Klotho protein, associated with aging processes.

**Why It Matters**

Higher Klotho levels are linked to extended lifespan and protection against age-related diseases.

### DNA Damage (8-OHdG)

**What It Measures**

Levels of 8-hydroxy-2′-deoxyguanosine, a marker of oxidative DNA damage.

**Why It Matters**

High levels suggest significant DNA damage, which can lead to mutations and disease development.

### Cytokines: IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8, TNF-α

**What It Measures**

Markers of systemic inflammation.

**Why It Matters**

Elevated levels indicate chronic inflammation, associated with various diseases and aging.

### Vitamin D

**What It Measures**

The concentration of vitamin D in your blood.

**Why It Matters**

Vitamin D is crucial for bone health, immune function, and inflammation regulation. Deficiency can lead to osteoporosis and increased infection risk.

### Glycated Serum Protein (GSP)

**What It Measures**

Average blood sugar levels over the past 1-2 weeks.

**Why It Matters**

Helps in monitoring short-term glucose control, crucial for diabetes management.

### Lipid panel: HDL, LDL, Triglycerides

**What It Measures**

Levels of various lipids in the blood.

**Why It Matters**

Imbalances can indicate cardiovascular risk, guiding dietary and lifestyle interventions.

### Uric Acid

**What It Measures**

Concentration of uric acid in the blood.

**Why It Matters**

High levels can lead to gout and are associated with metabolic syndrome. It is a very important longevity biomarker.

### Albumin

**What It Measures**

A protein produced by the liver, present in blood.

**Why It Matters**

Low levels may indicate liver or kidney issues.

### ALP (Alkaline Phosphatase) & ALT (Alanine Aminotransferase)

**What It Measures**

Enzymes related to liver function.

**Why It Matters**

Elevated levels can signal liver damage.

### Creatinine (CRE)

**What It Measures**

A molecule produced by muscle cells and filtered by the kidneys to maintain the normal levels.

**Why It Matters**

It is used to estimate glomerular filtration rate (eGFR). High levels can indicate impaired kidney function or increased muscle mass.

### Creatine Kinase (CK)

**What It Measures**

An enzyme found in muscles.

**Why It Matters**

Elevated CK can suggest muscle damage or stress.

### Hemoglobin A1c (HbA1C)

**What It Measures**

Your average blood sugar level over the past 2-3 months.

**Why It Matters**

It’s a crucial indicator of how well your blood sugar is being managed, especially for people with diabetes.

### D-Dimer

**What It Measures**

A protein fragment your body makes when a blood clot dissolves.

**Why It Matters**

Helps in determining if you may have a blood clotting condition and risk for stroke, heart attack or pulmonary embolism.

### Cystatin C

**What It Measures**

A protein made by cells in your body. Your kidneys filter out cystatin C to keep it at a normal level.

**Why It Matters**

Cystatin C is an alternative blood marker that increases as kidney function is impaired. In CKD, it is an alternative to serum creatinine for estimating glomerular filtration rate (eGFR). It may be better kidney biomarker than Creatinine in individuals with higher muscle mass or with creatine supplementation.

### ApoB

**What It Measures**

Apo B or Apolipoprotein B-100, which carries the bad cholesterol (LDL) in your blood that help make plaque, a waxy fat that can block your arteries.

**Why It Matters**

The test can tell you about your risk for cardiovascular (heart and blood vessel) disease, probably better than lipid panel.

### Homocysteine

**What It Measures**

Levels of an amino acid which is an intermediate in the metabolism of methionine and cysteine.

**Why It Matters**

Elevated homocysteine increases your risks for blood clots, heart disease and stroke. It is an indication that you may  lack vitamin B6, B12, folic acid or trimethylglycine (TMG). These vitamins are needed to break down homocysteine.

### Lp(a)

**What It Measures**

Apo B or Apolipoprotein B-100, which carries the bad cholesterol (LDL) in your blood that help make plaque, a waxy fat that can block your arteries.

**Why It Matters**

The test can tell you about your risk for cardiovascular (heart and blood vessel) disease, probably better than lipid panel.

### hs-CRP: high sensitivity C Reactive Protein

**What It Measures**

Markers of systemic inflammation.

**Why It Matters**

Elevated levels indicate chronic inflammation, associated with various diseases and aging.
