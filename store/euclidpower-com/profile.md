## Overview

Euclid Power is a renewable-energy project execution company that combines full-suite services with a software platform for development, diligence, transaction management, financing, compliance, construction, and asset work. It sells to developers, IPPs, operators, and infrastructure investors that need more structured project data and extra execution capacity. On April 30, 2026, Euclid announced its acquisition of Thresh Power, adding an AI-powered diligence platform to its software-and-services model.

## What they offer

- **Euclid Platform:** A "single source of truth for renewable energy projects and teams" that turns source documentation into costs, risks, requirements, action items, Workspace, Pipeline, Budget, Collaboration, and Doc Hub views; price not shown `[on-request]`.
- **Services suite:** Data & Document Organization, Bid Review, Portfolio Assessment, Transaction Management, Owner's Engineering, Financing Transaction Management, Development Partnership, and Full Service Program Management across develop, sell, buy, finance, build, and operate stages; price not shown `[on-request]`.
- **Red Flag Snapshot:** A 24-hour risk report and expert review for pre-bid diligence, including critical document gaps, project scorecard, prioritized risk register, RFI generation, open questions, and an expert call; price not shown `[on-request]`.
- **Tax Credit Compliance Services:** FEOC Compliance, Domestic Content Compliance, and PWA Compliance covering supplier screening, ownership/control diligence, MACR testing, supplier certifications, labor tracking, AUP reports, bringdowns, and audit-ready reporting; price not shown `[on-request]`.
- **Engineering & Construction:** Owner-side technical services from EPC RFP through COD, including full-service owner's engineering, milestone verification, document management, EPC coordination, punchlist, commissioning, closeout, SC Validation, Technical Reviews, Commissioning & Closeout, and Full Lifecycle OE; price not shown `[on-request]`.

## How it works / model

Euclid's service pages are consultative: users schedule a call or get in touch, then scope an engagement around a portfolio, transaction, compliance workstream, or construction stage. The Red Flag Snapshot page describes a concrete flow: talk to the team, share a data room through Dropbox/Box/SharePoint or another channel, Euclid uploads files to its platform, runs AI-assisted analysis, has an expert review findings, and delivers a shareable report plus a review call within 24 hours. The broader platform extracts project data and dates from documents, tracks action items, risks, RFIs, milestones, budgets, permissions, and documents in one workspace.

## Positioning & audience

Euclid positions itself as hands-on renewable-energy execution infrastructure, not just advisory: the site repeatedly says its team works "as an extension" of customers' teams and pairs domain experts with purpose-built software. The core audience is investors, developers, IPPs, operators, lenders, tax equity partners, and project stakeholders handling complex solar, storage, and distributed-energy portfolios. Its claimed edge is compressing project timelines, reducing risk, and replacing fragmented data rooms, spreadsheets, email, and project files with a single project record.

## Visual & brand impression

The brand reads like infrastructure software rather than climate consumer branding: dark navy backgrounds, thin circuit-grid linework, high-contrast white marks, neon cyan/yellow/purple labels, and pale product UI panels. The homepage and OG cover lean into system diagrams and project-flow schematics, while lower sections use clean enterprise case-study blocks and product screenshots. The visual message is "technical control room plus expert operators" more than generic clean-energy optimism.

## Strategic read

Euclid is deliberately hybrid: service lines sell expert execution, while the platform is framed as the system of record that makes those services faster and repeatable. The Thresh acquisition sharpens that into an AI-diligence story, especially around data-room extraction and renewables-specific analysis. The absence of published prices, paired with repeated schedule-call CTAs and named engagement tiers, makes the commercial motion look enterprise/services-led rather than self-serve SaaS.
