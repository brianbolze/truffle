What is Oral TRT?

A daily medication that helps restore testosterone to healthy levels, designed to fit into your morning routine.

How do I take it?

Take daily with a high-fat meal for optimal absorption, ideally with breakfast. Stay consistent for best results.

Common side effects

Oral TRT is generally well tolerated. The most commonly reported side effects include GI symptoms (nausea, upset stomach), headache, and increases in red blood cell count and blood pressure — both of which your provider will monitor with regular lab work. As with all testosterone therapies, rare but serious side effects are possible. Oral TRT is not recommended for men with uncontrolled high blood pressure. Your provider will review your complete health profile before prescribing and monitor your labs throughout treatment.

## Frequently  asked questions

How often do you take it?

Daily. Your doctor establishes dosage and timing based on your labs and lifestyle.

Does oral TRT affect fertility?

Like all forms of TRT, oral testosterone can suppress natural sperm production.

How fast does oral TRT work?

Can be faster than enclomiphene, but requires daily consistency. Most men feel initial improvements within weeks.

Who shouldn't use oral TRT?

Your doctor will screen for contraindications during consultation. Oral TRT isn't appropriate for everyone—some men need injectable options for absorption or dosing reasons.

How is progress monitored?

Regular bloodwork tracks your testosterone levels, along with other health markers. Your doctor adjusts dosing based on how you feel and what your labs show. All monitoring is included in your treatment cost. Plans start as low as $139/month.

Is oral testosterone hard on the liver?

Modern oral TRT formulations are designed to bypass the liver through lymphatic absorption, avoiding the liver toxicity concerns associated with older oral testosterone products.

Is treatment available in all states?

We're always working to add new states. Treatment is currently available as follows:

Enclomiphene: All states, excluding Louisiana

TRT: Not currently available in Alabama, Arkansas, Connecticut, Delaware, Georgia, Hawaii, Louisiana, Minnesota, Missouri, North Carolina, North Dakota, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, and South Carolina.
