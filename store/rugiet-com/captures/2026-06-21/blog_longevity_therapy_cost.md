## **How Much Does Longevity Therapy Actually Cost? NAD+, Sermorelin, Glutathione, and More**

_The price you see at a clinic and the price of the same therapy at home are often very different. Understanding why helps you make a better decision._

Cost is one of the first questions people have when they start exploring longevity therapies seriously, and it is one of the hardest to answer clearly — because pricing varies dramatically depending on format, provider type, and whether you are accessing therapy through a clinic or an at-home protocol.

This guide breaks down what actually drives the cost of the most common longevity therapies and what to expect at different price points.

## **What Actually Drives the Price of Longevity Therapy**

Before looking at specific therapies, it helps to understand the cost structure. Longevity therapy pricing reflects several factors that vary widely between providers:

- **Delivery format** — IV infusion at a clinic costs far more than subcutaneous injection at home, even if the compound is the same
- **Facility overhead** — clinic-based therapy includes real estate, nursing staff, and equipment that at-home protocols do not
- **Provider type** — a specialized longevity clinic charges more than a telehealth provider for equivalent access
- **Pharmacy source** — compounding pharmacy pricing for the same compound can vary significantly between suppliers
- **Protocol design** — a supervised protocol with monitoring and follow-up is priced differently than a one-time purchase

The most consistent pattern is that clinic-based IV delivery costs significantly more per dose than at-home subcutaneous delivery of the same compound. For ongoing longevity protocols, this difference compounds substantially over time.

## **NAD+ Therapy Cost**

NAD+ therapy spans the widest price range of any therapy in this space because it is available in so many formats.

IV NAD+ infusions at clinics represent the highest price point. A single session — typically one to four hours — is priced to reflect facility costs, nursing staff, and the premium attached to the clinical setting. For ongoing use, the cost adds up quickly.

Injectable NAD+ for at-home use is considerably more accessible. Pharmacy-grade subcutaneous injectable NAD+ through an at-home protocol delivers high bioavailability at a fraction of the per-dose cost of IV therapy. For people using NAD+ as part of a regular longevity protocol rather than for occasional acute sessions, at-home injection is where the value calculation clearly shifts.

Oral NMN and NR supplements are the lowest cost option but also the most variable in effectiveness. The bioavailability limitations of oral formats mean the effective cost per unit of NAD+ actually delivered to cells is less favorable than it appears at the shelf price.

## **Sermorelin Therapy Cost**

Sermorelin pricing varies most significantly based on provider type. A specialized men's health or longevity clinic will price sermorelin therapy with significant markups for in-person consultations, monitoring appointments, and the overhead of a physical practice.

Telehealth providers offering sermorelin prescriptions with pharmacy fulfillment are considerably more accessible. The compound itself — pharmacy-grade sermorelin for subcutaneous injection — is the same; what you are paying for at a clinic is access, not quality.

It is worth noting that sermorelin is a therapy designed for ongoing use over months, not a one-time treatment. The cost compounds over a protocol, which makes the provider and access model a more important decision than it might appear initially.

## **Glutathione Therapy Cost**

Glutathione pricing follows the same clinic-versus-at-home pattern as NAD+. IV glutathione at a clinic — often offered as an add-on to other IV therapy sessions — is priced at the high end, reflecting facility and administration costs.

Injectable glutathione for at-home subcutaneous use is substantially more affordable per dose. For people using glutathione regularly for recovery, skin health, or liver support, the at-home injection model makes a protocol that would be prohibitively expensive at IV clinic pricing genuinely sustainable.

## **Lipo-C and L-Carnitine Cost**

Lipo-C injections tend to be among the more accessible longevity therapies from a cost standpoint. The compounds — L-carnitine, choline, methionine, inositol, and B vitamins — are not exotic, and pharmacy-grade formulations are available at reasonable price points through providers who work with compounding pharmacies.

For people managing body composition and metabolic health, Lipo-C represents a strong cost-to-benefit ratio compared to pharmaceutical weight loss interventions, which often carry higher costs and more significant side effect considerations.

## **Peptide Therapy Cost**

Peptide therapy cost varies considerably by compound and protocol. Some peptides — sermorelin being the clearest example — are straightforward to access through telehealth and compounding pharmacy channels at accessible price points. Others are more specialized and priced accordingly.

The most important cost consideration with peptide therapy is ensuring you are not trading down on quality to save money. The purity and sterility of injectable peptides are not properties you can evaluate from price alone, and the cost of cutting corners on sourcing is not worth the savings.

## **Clinic vs At-Home: The Cost Gap**

- **NAD+**: High per session; IV format — More affordable; same pharmacy-grade quality
- **Sermorelin**: Higher with in-person overhead — Accessible via telehealth + pharmacy
- **Glutathione**: High as IV add-on — Sustainable for regular maintenance use
- **Lipo-C**: Variable by clinic — Generally accessible at-home
- **Peptides**: Varies by compound and protocol — Accessible with licensed provider

## **What to Look for Beyond the Price Tag**

Price matters, but a few other factors determine the actual value of a longevity therapy protocol.

Pharmacy source matters more than most people realize. Compounded medications from a regulated pharmacy with appropriate quality controls are not the same as compounds sourced from unregulated suppliers, even if the price is lower. The legitimacy of the pharmacy source is non-negotiable for anything you are injecting.

Provider oversight matters for getting results. A protocol with follow-up, dose adjustments, and monitoring produces better outcomes than a one-time purchase with no clinical guidance. The cheapest option is rarely the one with the most support.

Format affects value. A less expensive oral supplement that delivers limited bioavailability may have a worse effective cost than a slightly more expensive injectable that reliably delivers the compound to your cells.
