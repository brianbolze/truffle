BETA Begins Development

of its First eVTOL: AVA-XCThe BETA team embarks on the development of its first electric vertical takeoff and landing aircraft, **AVA-XC**. Weighing 4,000 pounds, AVA was created in partnership with **United Therapeutics** as an early prototype to demonstrate the potential of all-electric flight and lay the groundwork for future generations of BETA aircraft.

BETA Moves into its First

Space at Heritage AviationBETA establishes its first official workspace at **Heritage Aviation** in Burlington, Vermont. The early team begins building, testing, and flying in a shared hangar—laying the foundation for the company’s hands-on, pilot-driven approach to electric aviation.

![meet-ava-thumb](https://beta.team/hs-fs/hubfs/Photos/meet-ava-thumb.png?length=760&name=meet-ava-thumb.png)

Meet AVADesigned, built, tested, and flown within ten months by a handful of engineers, AVA was BETA’s first full-scale proof-of-concept (PoC) aircraft. AVA set a record as the heaviest eVTOL aircraft in the world to take flight. This design proved that electric aviation is possible, laying the foundation for the company to build on with future designs.

BETA’s Flight Training Program BeginsBETA offers every team member the opportunity to earn a pilot’s license. Flight school connects employees to the mission—giving them a common language, a deeper understanding of what they are designing, and a meaningful empathy for future operators. The program now includes a large team of flight instructors and a fleet of training aircraft, enabling training from a Private Pilot Certificate all the way up to an ATP.

Flight Test Operations Move

to Plattsburgh, New York
BETA establishes its flight test headquarters at **Plattsburgh International Airport (KPBG)**— just a 15-minute flight from the company’s R&D facility in South Burlington. Once home to B-52 bombers, the former Air Force base offers expansive hangar space, more than three miles of runway, and a supportive local community—making it the ideal location for BETA’s growing flight test program.

AVA's Teachings Inform ALIAThroughout the AVA program, the BETA team captured lessons about what would ultimately make electric aviation certifiable and commercially viable. These insights led the team to design and build a simpler, more refined aircraft: ALIA.

Design and Build of ALIA**ALIA** was inspired by the **Arctic Tern**, the farthest-migrating bird in the world. BETA’s engineers used biomimicry to guide key design choices, including ALIA’s 50-foot wingspan and large, sweeping tail section.

BETA takes a strategic approach to aircraft development—partnering with leaders across the aerospace supply chain for critical components while internally developing the proprietary electric propulsion system that powers its aircraft. The first-generation motor marks an important step in proving the performance and reliability of electric propulsion for aviation.

BETA Installs its First Prototype

Elevated Landing Pad and Pilot StationA crane positions the flight deck atop BETA’s first custom-built elevated landing pad. Constructed from recycled shipping containers and designed to operate off the grid, the pad offers a flexible solution for customers planning to use **ALIA** at off-airport locations such as distribution centers, office buildings, or hospitals. BETA’s First

Full-Scale SimulatorBETA’s first full-scale enclosed-dome simulator— **ThunderDome**—became an invaluable tool for flight testing, data collection, and pilot training. Every company test pilot flies full mission profiles in the simulator before replicating the same flight in the manned aircraft. Data from simulated flights informs software development across multiple systems, and the simulator itself runs in part on the popular home-simulation software **X-Plane**.
