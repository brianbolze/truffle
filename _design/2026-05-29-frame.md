# Frame: a global company-research engine

> **Goal.** Turn *"research company X"* into reliable, cached, structured intelligence that **any project can read and any agent can query** — Firecrawl for company-site capture, source-specific tools for repeatable external signals, Claude for reasoning, and a file-first store that warms with every use.

*Wallowing-stage Frame, per [Core: How We Work](https://www.notion.so/a4b0fd75ea984d66b8c3f849f1c8f838). Feels out the **problem** space — why, scope, the bets, the tensions — not the shape of the solution (that's the [Architecture](2026-05-30-architecture.md)). Name: **web-research**.*

## TL;DR

- **What.** A project-agnostic toolbox of company-research capabilities. The unit of reuse stops being "a Teleprescribe workflow" and becomes "a capability *any* project calls."
- **The bet.** Capture a company **once**, trustworthily — then **read it back**. For external signals, capture the source with a small reusable tool instead of generic web search. The moat is durable evidence plus a query layer agents actually reach for.
- **The payoff loop.** A company captured once (for Teleprescribe) is warm and nearly free for the next project (a new SaaS tool, a PE look, anything).
- **The discipline.** *Spend on conventions, not infrastructure.* Stay file-first; earn every piece of structure.

## Why not just use Claude's web search?

The fair challenge: Claude already has **WebSearch, Claude-in-Chrome, and deep-research**. Why build anything? Because *both halves* of researching a company are weak out of the box — the **fetch** and the **reasoning**.

- **The fetch is lossy and unreliable.** Generic web tools get bot-blocked, skip JavaScript-rendered content, *paraphrase* instead of quoting, and give no freshness guarantee. A purpose-built capture (Firecrawl) fixes all four — fresh, **verbatim**, JS-complete, bot-resistant. Trustworthy raw material is the precondition for everything downstream.
- **The reasoning is redone every time, then thrown away.** Deep-research reads raw and reasons *at query time* — ephemeral, hard to cite, no completeness guarantee, gone the moment it finishes. The engine structures **once, at ingestion**, into a cited dossier that every later read filters at ~$0.

> **The litmus test** (from agent-workflows): *"Is this making Claude materially more useful than someone with generic Claude + web search would have?"* A warm, structured, cited company store is exactly the thing a generic session doesn't have — and can't cheaply rebuild.

<details>
<summary>The deeper why — "AI at ingestion, structure for retrieval"</summary>

- **Do the AI work once, up front.** Doro's hard-won lesson: structuring and classifying *at ingestion* beats all reasoning at query time. The query can then *filter pre-structured data* — fast, complete, citable — instead of re-reading the web and hoping the model infers your categories the same way every time.
- **Query-time retrieval can't promise completeness.** "How do all 13 brands price X?" needs the *exhaustive* set; a top-k search returns passages that *sound like* the question, not the ones that *answer* it. Structure-at-ingestion gives completeness + precision; re-searching gives relevance + approximation.
- **The real failure of bought data was trust.** PE firms paid millions for Pitchbook / AlphaSense and called them "essentially useless" — not for lack of data, but lack of *provenance*. The lightweight answer here: **verbatim quotes, cited to the captured page.** A claim that survives the grep check can't be fabricated.

</details>

## Why this exists — two prior systems, each half-right

Given a *system* is worth building, the engine is the deliberate recombination of two prior ones, minus the weight of both.

| | Solved | Missed |
|---|---|---|
| **Doro** (PE tool) | AI-at-ingestion; provenance; freshness; structure-for-retrieval | heavy infra; entity-resolution; per-call LLM cost |
| **agent-workflows** | the light substrate — markdown · git · Claude | bound to one venture; **no query layer** |

The gap is the same in both: **no queryable, reusable company state.** Doro *could* query but was too heavy to live in. agent-workflows was light, but agents re-grepped from zero every run — and the failure was never "no database," it was **tooling + discoverability** on top of good files.

> **The one tension to manage forever.** Every new ambition — products, cross-brand aggregation, market maps — pulls *toward* Doro's heavy knowledge-graph. The substrate philosophy pulls *toward* lightness. **You already shut Doro down once.** When a decision smells heavy, that's the flag.

## The bet: capture once, well — then read it back

The leverage is two things the generic tools do *neither* of:

- **Capture *once*, trustworthily, and durably** — a Firecrawl pull for company-site State, plus focused source tools for repeatable external signals — so the expensive reasoning happens on stable evidence and is worth keeping.
- **Read it *back*** — the half agent-workflows never built: the query layer + discoverability that turn a folder of good files into something an agent actually reaches for.

Why it pays off: **warm once, reuse everywhere** — a company captured for one project is ~free for the next (Firecrawl is the only real cost; reasoning rides the Claude subscription). And the discipline that keeps it cheap: **spend on conventions, not infrastructure** — markdown is the source of truth; any index (e.g. a SQLite database) is a derived, regenerable lens.

## What the engine holds — and what it doesn't

The single line that decides where every fact lives. **Three kinds of fact, three homes:**

- **State** — what a company *is* right now: what it sells, who founded it, how it's positioned. A current **snapshot**, from the company's own pages. → **the store.**
- **Signals** — the same kind of fact on a **time axis**: funding rounds, traffic trend, headcount over time, review-count deltas, SERP movement. Dated, append-only. → **source-specific tools + the future signals layer** (some capture primitives now live in `tools/`; durable timelines remain an open design question — see [Architecture](2026-05-30-architecture.md)).
- **Judgments** — what a company *means to you*: formidable? a threat? a fit? Relative to who's asking. → **the project.**

> **A change is not a fourth bucket — it feeds the first two.** A product launch *updates the State snapshot* (they now sell X) **and** *appends a Signal* ("launched X in April"). Funding is almost *pure* Signal — its snapshot value ("total raised") is weak and stale-prone — which is exactly why the store, holding only snapshots, can't carry it well.

<details>
<summary>Why the split is load-bearing</summary>

- **Snapshots overwrite; signals append; judgments depend on the viewer.** Three different record shapes and lifetimes — collapsing them into one profile is the "ingest everything into a graph" move that made Doro heavy.
- **It keeps the warm cache trustworthy.** A funding field captured in May that says "last round 2022" looks current and is confidently *wrong* the day the company is recapitalized. State is safe to cache cheaply *because* it's a site-derived snapshot; signals are not.
- **The store is the priors layer, not the everything-bucket.** A deep-research run (news, funding, M&A, reviews) reads the store first, then goes wide and writes its narrative *project-side*, never into the profile. The store gives the foundation; the project keeps the story.

</details>

## How it stays light

- **Capabilities are global; opinions are local.** The engine owns generic, cross-domain classification. Market verticals ("Weight Loss") and all Judgments are project-owned, user-driven.
- **Domain is the key.** The canonical domain uniquely names a company — so entity resolution (Doro's most expensive subsystem) simply *disappears*. Rebrands and M&A are an escape hatch in the schema, not a resurrected embedding pipeline.

## Scope & phasing

> **v1 = on-demand single-company dossier.** `/research-company <domain>` → map → scrape key pages (cache-aware via freshness) → write `store/<domain>/profile.md`. **The store entry IS the dossier** — no separate artifact. A cold session reads that one file and answers.

| Phase | Delivers | Horizon |
|---|---|---|
| **Foundation** | Single-company dossier; query by frontmatter + a helper skill | now |
| **Aggregation** | Cross-company queries ("group Sermorelin across brands"); a derived index *only when* demanded | medium |
| **Visualization** | Market maps, 2×2s, battle maps (crowdedness / dominance / hotness) | long |

*Visualizations are pull-consumers — but they impose requirements backward: hotness needs **Signals**, so the signals layer is a first-class *future*, not a bolt-on, even though the maps themselves are far off.*

## Non-goals — the anti-Doro guardrails

If a decision pushes toward any of these, we're rebuilding the thing we sunset:

- Not real-time. Not a served API / webhooks.
- Not a graph DB (Neo4j); no embeddings / vector RAG.
- No private-source ingestion (Gong/Slack).
- Not multi-tenant. Not a *served* product — no hosting, no accounts; the engine produces artifacts, it doesn't serve them ([presentation lens](2026-06-09-presentation-lens.md)).
- No company *discovery* or identity-validation scoring — you hand it a domain.
- No datapoint store / reconciliation engine.
- **The engine owns no *vertical* taxonomy.** Market verticals (e.g. "Weight Loss") are project-owned, user-driven. It ships only a few *generic, cross-domain* closed sets (industry, offering, market type — see [`TAXONOMIES.md`](../TAXONOMIES.md)).

## Success criteria

- A cold Cowork session answers *"how do the top N telehealth brands describe + price Sermorelin?"* with cited, fresh data in **one invocation, no hand-holding.**
- A second project hitting an already-captured company pays **~$0 Firecrawl.**
- The store is **legible enough** that a deep-research agent reads it before re-scraping.
- *(Long-term)* a category emits a **market-map-ready dataset** (players × prominence × hotness).
- Brian trusts it enough to use in real brand/strategy calls (the agent-workflows bar).

## Principles

- **Spend on conventions, not infrastructure.**
- **Capabilities are global; opinions — including vertical taxonomies — are project-local.**
- **Derive, don't store**, anything the domain gives you for free.
- **Capture observations, not interpretations — verbatim is gold.** Quote what the page says; don't assert what it means. A verbatim claim can't be fabricated past the grep check, so it's the engine's natural anti-hallucination guard. (Volatile facts are grep-or-`unverified`; the rare prior lands only marked — see SCHEMA.)
- **Every field is a cut.** A categorical field earns its place by *how well it divides companies on a question you actually ask* — information gain, not completeness. The worked test (universal-vs-module, when a cut belongs to a cohort) lives in [`SCHEMA.md`](../SCHEMA.md).
- **Name for what it is**, not what it might become — let "Market Intelligence" be earned.
