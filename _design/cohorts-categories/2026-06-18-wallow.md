# Wallow: cohorts and categories

Date: 2026-06-18 - Status: idea / wallow note, **not a frame**.

> **Purpose.** Capture the spark, motivations, prior art, and early problem decomposition for first-class category / cohort intelligence in Truffle. This is input for discussion, not a proposal. A concise problem frame should come later and live beside this note.

## The spark

The trigger was a fresh capture of Waldo (`waldo.fyi`), a tool adjacent to Truffle but aimed at marketing and agency strategy. The first pass showed Waldo packaging one normalized data layer into workflows like Strategize, Pitch, Monitor, and Build. That packaging is interesting, but it is probably too far up the ladder for Truffle right now.

The more immediate pull is lower-level: **depth and ingredients**.

Waldo's site repeatedly sells the quality of its inputs: live social, ads, audience data, trends, events, open web, source-linked outputs, and workflows grounded in repeatable analyses rather than generic LLM generation. The relevant question for Truffle is not "how do we package workflows like Waldo?" It is: **what are the best source ingredients we should be capturing, at the right grain, so future synthesis has better raw material?**

That led back to a bigger tension that has been on Brian's mind: **category / cohort intelligence**.

Today Truffle is company-keyed. The canonical domain is the core identity anchor, which has been a huge simplification. But it also creates a blind spot: to understand a company, you often need to understand its neighborhood.

## The felt gap

Company profiles answer "what is this company?" reasonably well. They do not naturally answer the market questions around it:

- **Relations / neighborhood.** Who are the nearby players, substitutes, competitors, parents, partners, and similar companies?
- **Membership / coverage.** What category does this company participate in, and which relevant companies are missing from the store?
- **Pattern / differentiation.** What claims, offers, pricing moves, UX patterns, and acquisition surfaces are becoming normal, and is this company actually differentiated?
- **Source panel.** Which sources define the market: SERPs, listicles, ads, Reddit, review surfaces, app stores, regulatory surfaces, marketplaces?
- **News / change pulse.** What is happening in this company's market before it resolves into one company's profile update?

This is an active pain point in Telehealth work. The company profile is useful, but the strategic read often depends on cross-company context: treatment lines, molecules, offer structures, pharmacy posture, intake models, pricing visibility, claims language, category SERPs, and market-entry signals.

The current cohort packs only scratch the surface. A cohort pack extends a company profile with vertical-specific fields (`store/<domain>/telehealth.md`), but it is still **per-company State**. It does not capture category-grain State or Signals directly.

Earlier architecture notes had already left a placeholder for this:

- `store/<domain>/` = company-grain State and company-grain Signals.
- `modules/cohort-packs/` = per-company vertical-specific cuts.
- `cohorts/<category-slug>/` = reserved later home for cross-company / category-grain signals.

That placeholder is now becoming a real problem area, not just a future note.

## Why company-keying worked

Company-domain keying dodged a lot of Doro's hardest problems.

Domains provide a mostly stable, externally verifiable key:

```text
domain -> company profile
```

That means Truffle could avoid a heavy entity-resolution system. The engine could be file-first, source-cited, and practical. Capture a domain once, read it back later.

But a category has no equivalent free key. There is no universal, self-evident name for "medical weight loss," "men's health," "longevity medicine," "DTC telehealth," "brand intelligence," or "market research software."

It isn't all-or-nothing, though. The things we want to capture sit on a spectrum of how easy they are to pin down:

- Some still ride the company key we already have — "Hims launched X," a funding round, a price move.
- Some aren't a company but still have a clear name the world agrees on — a molecule, an FDA rule, a specific listicle page.
- And some have no clean name at all — the broad category labels above (e.g. "men's health")

The trouble lives almost entirely in that last group. That's where keying reopens everything company domains let us dodge: naming, boundaries, membership, overlap, granularity, stale membership, and sprawl. So a better opening question than "how do we key categories?" might be: how much of what we want actually needs that fuzzy layer at all, versus riding keys we already have?

## Why this is hard

The hard part is not making a folder called `cohorts/`. Two deeper difficulties sit underneath it.

**First, "category intelligence" is several jobs wearing one label.** Relations/navigation, membership/boundary work, source panels, pattern extraction, differentiation reads, and news/change pulse are related, but they do not have the same evidence bar or freshness need. Watching change can tolerate fuzzy edges; defining membership is mostly about the edges. Treating all of this as one problem is part of what makes it feel so hard.

**Second, deciding which kinds of things are allowed to become _durable enough to index_.**

Open tensions:

- **Reference resolution.** "Hims," "Hims & Hers," and "ForHims" are the easy version: they probably resolve to one company/domain. The harder version is when sources say "weight loss," "obesity care," "GLP-1 telehealth," or "men's health" and we have to decide whether to point that at a topic, cohort, market, offering attribute, or just the source's own wording.
- **Category naming.** Different sources use adjacent labels for the same neighborhood. Do we treat them as synonyms, overlapping lenses, or separate markets?
- **Level of analysis.** The useful market object may be broad and conventional ("medical weight loss," "men's health"), while narrower concepts may be better handled as attributes, offerings, or query-time groupings.
- **Membership.** Who belongs in a cohort, who is a candidate, and who is explicitly excluded?
- **Cohort sprawl.** A useful cohort can multiply into dozens of adjacent slices and multiple layers of granularity. Without discipline, the category layer becomes an ontology project.
- **Use-case relativity.** A cohort useful for Telehealth strategy may not be useful for a different project. Relevance is not universal.
- **Source grain mismatch.** SERP results are query-grain, ads are advertiser/domain-grain, Reddit is thread/comment-grain, Wayback is URL-grain, offerings are company/product-grain. Collapsing them too early creates false confidence.
- **False positives vs. false negatives.** Bad members pollute cross-company reads; missing members hide trends and new entrants.
- **Lifecycle.** Category membership changes. New players enter, offers disappear, claims rotate, listicles update, and SERPs move.

The problem is partly "how do we capture category signals?" but more fundamentally: **what entities get persisted in Truffle, which entities get structured artifacts, and what stays a run-specific grouping or judgment?**

## The persistence question

The existing **State / Signals / Judgments** split is the strongest current lean, and the Signals / traction work is the nearest storage precedent: persist the smallest durable evidence object that makes future reads cheaper and more trustworthy, and defer the more opinionated layer until a real consumer earns it. But that is a lean, not a settled constraint. For Signals, it has meant:

- raw tool envelopes before normalized "cards",
- a path convention before a schema/lint/writer stack,
- per-axis facts before blended scores,
- facts in the engine, verdicts with the project,
- grain stamped on every comparison so one signal cannot masquerade as another.

See [2026-06-15-traction-approach.md](../2026-06-15-traction-approach.md) for more detail.

This discipline is a useful lens for cohorts and categories, but there are two coupled choices:

- **What gets durable identity?** Entities like categories/cohorts, relations like "competitors", and evidence objects like query panels/source captures are different things. Two gates matter: can we name it cleanly without spiraling into an ontology project, and does it stay true long enough to be worth coming back to? A rule change may be cleanly named but belong as a dated event; a SERP panel may be useful but stale immediately.
- **What gets structured artifacts?** A category signal can persist without automatically minting a durable category entity or producing a `profile.md`-style artifact.

That matters because Truffle already persists Signals, but today those signals are mostly **for companies**. A category SERP, listicle panel, ads snapshot, or news/event capture can be durable evidence without automatically minting a durable category entity.

Possible starting biases:

| Thing | Default | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Query-time grouping** | Do not persist as entity | Good default for one-off market questions. Name it, cite it, and caveat coverage, but do not turn it into an index object. |
| **Source capture / event** | Persist when repeatable | Useful when costly, source-linked, or valuable for change over time: SERPs, listicles, ads, news, Wayback, reviews. Stamp the grain and date. |
| **Relations / membership** | Persist only when useful and typed | Relations may cover membership, competitors, similar companies, parents/subsidiaries, and partnerships. The hard part is preserving relation type, evidence, and confidence. |
| **Category / cohort entity** | Rarely at first | Persist only when the market grain is stable enough to name without creating a taxonomy project. |
| **Structured category artifact** | Later, earned | Produce one when a durable entity has repeated consumers. Do not create a `profile.md` equivalent just because a signal exists. |
| **Judgment / score** | Keep project-side | "Dominant," "hot," "threat," and "worth entering" are viewer-relative. |

So the first design question is not "how do we build cohorts?" It is: **what entity or artifact, if any, deserves to outlive this run?**

Relations are probably the nearest high-priority sibling to cohorts. They are useful before a full category layer exists: HTML briefs could link to related company briefs, and the Telehealth Pantry could write back competitor / related-company references to the Organizations database. This argues against collapsing relation types into one bucket: ownership, partnership, source-attested competitor, algorithmic similarity, and category membership have different evidence bars and different lifetimes.

## Query-time grouping

This feels like the main pressure valve.

Before creating a durable category/cohort object, Truffle may be able to answer many market questions through **query-time grouping**: a temporary, explicit grouping assembled for the question at hand from company profiles, offerings, signals, and source captures.

Examples:

- "Group the companies that currently sell medical weight loss programs."
- "Compare pricing visibility across men's health brands."
- "Find the companies that appear repeatedly in DTC telehealth SERPs."
- "Show common claims across the longevity / age-management neighborhood."

The grouping is still named and cited, but it is not yet stored as a category. The important caveat is coverage: what companies were visible to the store, what source surfaces were checked, and what likely misses or stale fields could make the answer incomplete.

A grouping might graduate only when it repeats:

- the same grouping is requested multiple times,
- the boundary becomes stable enough to write down,
- there is a recurring source-capture need at that grain,
- and the value is not recoverable with a one-off query over company records.

This may be the simplest bridge from today's company-keyed store to tomorrow's category intelligence.

## Prior art, scoped correctly

Doro is prior art, but not the blueprint. It was going after **product / feature-level market intelligence** and an entity-centric datapoint architecture for VC / PE-style research.

This thread is different. The pull now is more conventional market analysis: categories, neighborhoods, competitors, source surfaces, and category-level movement. The Doro lesson is not "build the Doro graph again." It is: **be precise about what earns durable identity, because it can get messy fast.** Cleanup gets expensive once bad objects enter the index, and achieving sufficient coverage, depth, and freshness in a sprawling index gets expensive.

Local prior art now lives in [`prior-art/`](prior-art/):

- [`tech-blog-post.md`](prior-art/tech-blog-post.md) - Doro datapoint architecture: AI at ingestion, provenance, reconciliation, structured retrieval.
- [`doro-product-analysis-prompt.md`](prior-art/doro-product-analysis-prompt.md) - product portfolio and breadth-first granularity guidance.
- [`doro-code/`](prior-art/doro-code/) - company/product schemas, reference resolution, product normalization, candidate validation.
- [Traction frame](../2026-06-14-traction-frame.md), [Traction approach](../2026-06-15-traction-approach.md), and [SIGNALS](../../SIGNALS.md) - the current engine precedent for capture -> persist -> diff, raw envelopes, grain discipline, and no scores.

Carry forward:

- **AI at ingestion, structure for retrieval.** Do the expensive interpretation when evidence is captured, not every time a question is asked.
- **Provenance first.** The output needs receipts, not just a confident synthesis.
- **Breadth beats premature depth.** Doro's product prompt warned against listing 20 variants of one line while missing the rest of the portfolio. The same applies to markets: do not split a category before you understand the neighborhood.
- **Resolution and normalization are different jobs.** Matching a mention to a known object is not the same as deciding what label or category vocabulary to use.
- **Candidates leak.** Any discovery system needs a way to mark candidates, exclusions, stale items, and bad matches without pretending the index is clean forever.

Do not port:

- A product/feature canonical graph.
- A universal category ontology.
- A datapoint/reconciliation engine.
- Embedding-led entity resolution as the default move.
- Multi-tenant/API infrastructure.

Waldo remains the trigger, not the template. Its useful lesson is that source ingredients and receipts are the asset. Its workflow packaging, exact source mix, and MCP/API posture are later problems.

## Early use cases

Keep these as discussion prompts, not scope.

- **News / change pulse.** What recent launches, partnerships, M&A, regulatory events, or market moves matter before they resolve into a profile update? "News" is not one thing: company events, named non-company events, and broad topic news probably belong in different places.
- **Relations / navigation.** What companies and substitutes sit near this company, what evidence says so, and which should become jump targets in HTML briefs or Telehealth Notion "Organizations" rows (the "pantry")?
- **Membership audit.** Who is definitely in the cohort, who is a candidate, and who is explicitly out?
- **Category source panel.** Which sources define this market right now: SERPs, listicles, ads, reviews, forums, marketplaces, regulatory surfaces?
- **Pattern extraction.** What offers, claims, pricing moves, and UX patterns are becoming common across the neighborhood?

## Source ingredients

The "best farm" question should stay concrete: which source surfaces are repeatable, source-linkable, cheap enough, and useful at market/category grain?

- **Search surfaces** - category SERPs, AI Overview presence, recurring domains, query intent.
- **Listicles / directories** - third-party pages that define the market set, with rank and wording.
- **Ads transparency** - active or recent paid acquisition, claims, and landing pages.
- **News / announcements** - company newsrooms, press releases, credible coverage, launches, partnerships, M&A, regulatory events.
- **Wayback** - category page emergence, offer-page changes, disclaimer shifts.
- **Reviews / forums** - recurring mentions and language; probably not sentiment scoring at first.
- **Regulatory / source-of-truth surfaces** - category-dependent, especially for Telehealth.
- **Relationship surfaces** - owned "alternatives" pages, partner pages, integrations, clinical partners, pharmacies, affiliates, and customers.

Open question: which surfaces help define membership, which expose missing companies, and which only provide weak context?

## Things this should not become too early

Beyond the Doro machinery already ruled out under *Do not port* above, three traps are specific to this layer:

- A generic market score.
- A fully automated category-discovery system.
- A place where project-specific judgments leak into shared State.

## Discussion prompts

- What are the 2-3 Telehealth market questions that company profiles currently make painful?
- For each question, what would actually need to persist: no entity, raw source capture, membership relation, category/cohort entity, structured artifact, or project judgment?
- Which questions can query-time grouping answer without creating a stored cohort?
- When does a repeated grouping deserve to graduate into a durable cohort?
- Which relation types matter first, and what evidence/freshness makes them safe enough to show or write back?
- Where does news belong — and is it even one question? (Company events, named things like rules and approvals, and broad topic news may each belong somewhere different.)
- What is the right conventional market grain for the first real example: DTC telehealth, medical weight loss, men's health, longevity, or something else?
- Which sources define membership versus merely providing color?
- What coverage caveats or anti-evidence keep a market answer from sounding more complete than it is?
- Which parts are engine-generic, and which parts should remain project-owned?
