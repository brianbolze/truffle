# Wallow: cohorts and categories

Date: 2026-06-18 - Status: idea / wallow note, **not a frame**.

> **Purpose.** Capture the spark, motivations, prior art, and early problem decomposition for first-class category / cohort intelligence in Truffle. This is input for discussion, not a proposal. A concise problem frame should come later and live beside this note.

## The spark

The trigger was a fresh capture of Waldo (`waldo.fyi`), a tool adjacent to Truffle but aimed at marketing and agency strategy. The first pass showed Waldo packaging one normalized data layer into workflows like Strategize, Pitch, Monitor, and Build. That packaging is interesting, but it is probably too far up the ladder for Truffle right now.

The more immediate pull is lower-level: **depth and ingredients**.

Waldo's site repeatedly sells the quality of its inputs: live social, ads, audience data, trends, events, open web, source-linked outputs, and workflows grounded in repeatable analyses rather than generic LLM generation. The relevant question for Truffle is not "how do we package workflows like Waldo?" It is: **what are the best source ingredients we should be capturing, at the right grain, so future synthesis has better raw material?**

That led back to a bigger tension that has been on Brian's mind: **category / cohort intelligence**.

Today Truffle is company-keyed. The canonical domain is the core identity anchor, which has been a huge simplification. But it also creates a blind spot: to understand a company, you often need to understand its neighborhood.

## The felt gap

Company profiles answer "what is this company?" reasonably well. They do not naturally answer:

- What category does this company actually participate in?
- Who are the nearby players and substitutes?
- What claims, offers, pricing moves, UX patterns, and acquisition surfaces are becoming normal in the neighborhood?
- Which sources define the category: SERPs, listicles, ads, Reddit, review surfaces, app stores, regulatory surfaces, marketplaces?
- What category-level movement is happening even before it resolves into one company's profile?
- Which companies should be in the competitive set but are missing from the store?

This is an active pain point in Telehealth work. The company profile is useful, but the strategic read often depends on cross-company context: treatment lines, molecules, offer structures, pharmacy posture, intake models, pricing visibility, claims language, category SERPs, and market-entry signals.

The current cohort packs only scratch the surface. A cohort pack extends a company profile with vertical-specific fields (`store/<domain>/telehealth.md`), but it is still **per-company State**. It does not capture category-grain State or Signals directly.

The architecture already reserves the idea:

- `store/<domain>/` = company-grain State and company-grain Signals.
- `modules/cohort-packs/` = per-company vertical-specific cuts.
- `cohorts/<category-slug>/` = reserved later home for cross-company / category-grain signals.

That reserved space is now becoming a real problem area, not just a future note.

## Why company-keying worked

Company-domain keying dodged a lot of Doro's hardest problems.

Domains provide a mostly stable, externally verifiable key:

```text
domain -> company profile
```

That means Truffle could avoid a heavy entity-resolution system. The engine could be file-first, source-cited, and practical. Capture a domain once, read it back later.

But a category has no equivalent free key.

There is no universal, self-evident key for:

- "Medical weight loss"
- "Men's health"
- "Longevity medicine"
- "DTC telehealth"
- "Brand intelligence"
- "Market research software"

The moment we key on categories, we reopen the issues company domains let us dodge: naming, boundaries, membership, overlap, granularity, stale membership, and sprawl.

## Why this is hard

The hard part is not making a folder called `cohorts/`. The hard part is deciding what kinds of objects are allowed to become durable enough to index.

Open tensions:

- **Reference resolution.** When a source says "Hims," "Hims & Hers," or "ForHims," which company/domain should that mention point to?
- **Category naming.** Different sources use adjacent labels for the same neighborhood. Do we treat them as synonyms, overlapping lenses, or separate markets?
- **Level of analysis.** The useful market object may be broad and conventional ("medical weight loss," "men's health"), while narrower concepts may be better handled as attributes, offerings, or query-time groupings.
- **Membership.** Who belongs in a cohort, who is a candidate, and who is explicitly excluded?
- **Cohort sprawl.** A useful cohort can multiply into dozens of adjacent slices. Without discipline, the category layer becomes an ontology project.
- **Use-case relativity.** A cohort useful for Telehealth strategy may not be useful for a different project. Relevance is not universal.
- **Source grain mismatch.** SERP results are query-grain, ads are advertiser/domain-grain, Reddit is thread/comment-grain, Wayback is URL-grain, offerings are company/product-grain. Collapsing them too early creates false confidence.
- **False positives vs. false negatives.** Bad members pollute cross-company reads; missing members hide trends and new entrants.
- **Lifecycle.** Category membership changes. New players enter, offers disappear, claims rotate, listicles update, and SERPs move.

The problem is partly "how do we capture category signals?" but more fundamentally: **how do we prevent category intelligence from pulling Truffle back into Doro's heavy knowledge graph problem?**

## Prior art, scoped correctly

Doro is prior art, but not the blueprint. It was going after **product / feature-level market intelligence** and an entity-centric datapoint architecture for PE-style research. That made sense for software and tech markets, where product and feature comparison is often the analysis unit.

This thread is different. The pull now is more conventional market analysis: categories, neighborhoods, competitors, source surfaces, and category-level movement. The Doro lesson is not "build the Doro graph again." It is: **be precise about what earns durable identity, because cleanup gets expensive once bad objects enter the index.**

Local prior art now lives in [`prior-art/`](prior-art/):

- [`tech-blog-post.md`](prior-art/tech-blog-post.md) - Doro datapoint architecture: AI at ingestion, provenance, reconciliation, structured retrieval.
- [`doro-product-analysis-prompt.md`](prior-art/doro-product-analysis-prompt.md) - product portfolio and breadth-first granularity guidance.
- [`doro-code/`](prior-art/doro-code/) - company/product schemas, reference resolution, product normalization, candidate validation.

Carry forward:

- **AI at ingestion, structure for retrieval.** Do the expensive interpretation when evidence is captured, not every time a question is asked.
- **Provenance first.** The output needs receipts, not just a confident synthesis.
- **Breadth beats premature depth.** Doro's product prompt warned against listing 20 variants of one line while missing the rest of the portfolio. The same applies to markets: do not split a category before you understand the neighborhood.
- **Resolution and normalization are different jobs.** Matching a mention to a known object is not the same as deciding what label or category vocabulary to use.
- **Candidates leak.** Any discovery system needs a way to mark candidates, exclusions, stale items, and bad matches without pretending the index is clean forever.

Do not port:

- A product/feature canonical graph.
- A universal category ontology.
- A datapoint/reconciliation engine.
- Embedding-led entity resolution as the default move.
- Multi-tenant/API infrastructure.

Waldo's useful lessons for this discussion:

- It sells a data layer before it sells AI.
- It emphasizes source-linked "receipts."
- It combines several source families: social, ads, audience panels, trends, events, open web.
- It frames recurring scans by category / audience / brand / trend.
- It exposes data through app, chat connectors, API, and MCP.

What not to overlearn:

- Their workflow packaging is later-stage for Truffle.
- Their exact source set is marketing-specific.
- Their "10,000 agents" posture is positioning, not an architectural pattern.
- Their Build/API/MCP packaging does not answer Truffle's underlying category-key problem.

The more relevant prompt is: **which category-level sources produce durable, citable ingredients Truffle cannot get from company profiles alone?**

## Query-time grouping

This feels worth elevating.

Before creating a durable category/cohort object, Truffle may be able to answer many market questions through **query-time grouping**: a temporary, explicit grouping assembled for the question at hand from company profiles, offerings, signals, and source captures.

That gives us a pressure valve between "everything is company-keyed" and "we now have a category index." A grouping can exist for a run, a brief, or a project discussion without becoming a durable object in the shared store.

Examples:

- "Group the companies that currently sell medical weight loss programs."
- "Compare pricing visibility across men's health brands."
- "Find the companies that appear repeatedly in DTC telehealth SERPs."
- "Show common claims across the longevity / age-management neighborhood."

The grouping is still named and cited, but it is not yet a stored category. It can carry caveats: query terms used, companies included, companies excluded, and source surfaces checked.

A grouping might graduate into a real cohort only when it repeats:

- the same grouping is requested multiple times,
- the boundary becomes stable enough to write down,
- there is a recurring source-capture need at that grain,
- and the value is not recoverable with a one-off query over company records.

This may be the simplest bridge from today's company-keyed store to tomorrow's category intelligence.

## Early use cases

Keep these as discussion prompts, not scope.

- **Competitive neighborhood.** What companies and substitutes sit near this company, and what evidence says so?
- **Membership audit.** Who is definitely in the cohort, who is a candidate, and who is explicitly out?
- **Category source panel.** Which sources define this market right now: SERPs, listicles, ads, reviews, forums, marketplaces, regulatory surfaces?
- **Pattern extraction.** What offers, claims, pricing moves, and UX patterns are becoming common across the neighborhood?
- **Change detection.** What changed at the category level before it shows up as a single-company profile update?

## Source ingredients to examine

The "best farm" question should stay concrete. Which source surfaces are repeatable, source-linkable, cheap enough, and useful at market/category grain?

- **Search surfaces** - category SERPs, AI Overview presence, recurring domains, query intent.
- **Listicles / directories** - third-party pages that define the market set, with rank and wording.
- **Ads transparency** - active or recent paid acquisition, claims, and landing pages.
- **Wayback** - category page emergence, offer-page changes, disclaimer shifts.
- **Reviews / forums** - recurring mentions and language; probably not sentiment scoring at first.
- **Regulatory / source-of-truth surfaces** - category-dependent, especially for Telehealth.
- **Owned comparison pages** - "alternatives," "compare," "best for" pages that reveal perceived competitors.

Open question: which surfaces help define membership, and which only provide weak context?

## Things this should not become too early

- A universal category ontology.
- A product/feature intelligence dataset at Doro scale.
- A graph database or embedding-led resolution system.
- A generic market score.
- A fully automated category discovery system.
- A place where project-specific judgments leak into shared State.
