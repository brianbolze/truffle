# Retro — offerings module, Stripe run (2026-06-04)

First **metered / usage rate-card** `Catalog` ([`store/stripe-com/offerings.md`](../../../store/stripe-com/offerings.md): 5 families → ~30 product leaves, lint-clean). Unlike airbnb/notion, slug + roster held *well* (real product URLs, `/pricing` a true priced backbone) — the strain moved entirely onto **Price**.

- **Price (verbatim) is the tear — a rate-card flattened into a string.** Stripe prices are per-unit tables, not numbers: a base rate + N surcharges + alternative models (Payments `2.9% + 30¢ (+0.5% manual, +1.5% intl, +1% FX)`; Terminal stacks txn-% + reader `$59/$299` + cellular `$10/mo`; Billing is two parallel models — `$620/mo` **or** `0.7% of volume`). One cell *can't* hold that faithfully; I kept it lint-legal by quoting the **headline unit rate verbatim + parenthesized surcharges**, then offloading tiers/alt-models to `### Verbatim anchors`. **Held the lint, broke the premise** that "a price" is a value.
- **Visibility under-discriminated, and mis-answers for a metered seller.** ~28/30 rows are trivially `published` (every list price is shown), so the "can I get a price?" axis barely fires. But the *real* price for any enterprise is **Custom IC+ "Contact sales"** — and that's a **company-level tier, not a row**, so it falls out of the per-SKU model entirely. Visibility says "list shown" (true) but can't say "list ≠ what you'll pay" (also true). The `Included with Payments` rows took `published` with no own price — same parent-price stretch notion/airbnb hit.
- **Added no column — right call, but the meta-signal is now loud.** Folded pricing-basis (`per-txn` / `subscription` / `bundled`) into `What` and the rate-card into a `### Verbatim anchors` "Included with Payments" note; resisted a `Pricing model` column. But that's **three non-Rx runs, three different wanted optional axes** — notion's `Category`, airbnb's `Status`, my `pricing-basis`. The generalizable finding isn't any one of them; it's that **the spine needs one blessed optional project-column slot**, not three bespoke names.
- **Where OFFERINGS.md was silent — the exact guesses.** (1) **A compound/tiered/metered price in one `Price (verbatim)` cell** — the contract assumes a price is *a* value ("quoted exactly, with its footnote markers, or `—`"); silent on a rate card. → guessed headline-rate-in-cell, full-table-in-anchors. (2) **`Included with Payments`** bundled rows — no token for "priced into the parent's rate" (notion's `incl.` gap, unfixed). (3) **The lint only greps `$` amounts** — so `2.9% + 30¢`, `0.7%`, `3.5%` (i.e. *most* of a metered company's price signal) ride **unverified** by rule 4. That's a real integrity hole for this shape, not just an ergonomics gap.
- **One change.** Add a **"metered / rate-card shape" note** to OFFERINGS.md: Price cell carries the **headline unit rate verbatim + pointer**, the full card (surcharges, tiers, alt-models) lives in `### Verbatim anchors` keyed by offering — don't cram the sub-table into the cell; bless an `Included (parent)` / `bundled` token; and **extend the lint's grep guard to `%`/`¢` amounts**, not just `$`, so the rate card is actually integrity-checked.
- **Verdict.** **Fit — strongest structural fit of the three non-Rx runs** (~85%: slug real, roster a genuine indexed backbone, lint green), but the metered Price cell is a table pretending to be a string and the lint guards only its `$` slice. The module stretched without tearing; one contract note + a one-line lint change (grep `%`/`¢`) close the gap before the next usage-priced run.
