# Retro — offerings module, Ford run (2026-06-04)

First **`Multi-product` physical-goods** run (vehicles) — dealer-gated pricing + a financing line. ~22 model lines off
one priced index (`/showroom`), 5 flagship deep blocks w/ hero renders
([`store/ford-com/offerings.md`](../../../store/ford-com/offerings.md), lint-clean). Closest non-Rx fit to the telehealth
grain yet: an enumerable, published-price roster.

- **Spine held semantically; two stretches.** `Price (verbatim)` = real greppable MSRPs; `molecule · form · access`
  reframed to **`body group · powertrain · access`** and the molecule-attestation rule *generalized perfectly* — I wrote
  `powertrain: not stated` for ~8 models with no captured PDP rather than infer Maverick's standard hybrid etc. (the
  exact "never infer from the brand name" discipline, applied to drivetrains). The stretches: (1) **`Slug` stopped being a
  key** for the 9 commercial chassis — no marketing landing exists, so they all share `/new-commercial-trucks`; non-unique,
  the lint only checks attested+non-blank so it passed, but "slug = within-company key" degraded to "attested source page."
  (2) **`Kind` = `buyable` at model grain** — but a vehicle model *is* a family (it nests trims), so I flattened the
  family/buyable axis and pushed the trim ladder to deep blocks.
- **Added nothing to the spine — right call.** `Parent` carried the `/showroom` category grouping (SUVs/Trucks/Cars/
  Electric/Commercial/Performance), incl. cross-listed rows (`SUVs · Electric` for Mach-E); the `[HIGH/MED/LOW]` prominence
  read is already sanctioned. No new column earned. Nothing to promote.
- **OFFERINGS.md silent spot — `Visibility` for a dealer-set final price.** The page shows a manufacturer **starting MSRP**
  (published floor) but the real transaction price is dealer-set (adjustment + destination + fees, per the S1 footnote) and
  balloons with trims. The `partial` tree literally includes *"a floor that moves materially with dose/tier"* — by that
  reading vehicle trims would force `partial` on every row. I ruled **`published`**: the shown number is a real,
  self-contained *base-vehicle* price, and the add-ons are a **third-party dealer markup / external fees**, not a hidden
  *mandatory cost from the same seller* (the telehealth `partial` case). The doc gives no steer on this maker-MSRP-vs-
  dealer-final distinction — I guessed.
- **One change (capture recipe / lint, not the schema).** `offeringscheck.py`'s trailing-digit guard `(?!\d)` **can't
  verify a price the page glued to a footnote marker** — Ford renders `$79,0051` (= "$79,005" + ¹), comma-stripped to
  `$790051`, so `79005(?!\d)` never matches. Cost me **3 real halo MSRPs** (F-150 Raptor, Mustang Dark Horse SC, Bronco
  Raptor) — described qualitatively instead. Footnoted prices are universal in regulated/auto/finance copy; the lint should
  tolerate a 1–2 digit trailing footnote (e.g. allow `$NUM` followed by a lone super­script-class digit, or match the
  `S1`/`¹` family). Highest-leverage fix of the run.
- **Trims did *not* explode the grain — `/showroom` handed me the indexed level.** Roster stayed at **model** grain
  (~22 rows) because the company's own index does; trim ladders (F-150's 8, Mustang's EcoBoost→Dark Horse SC) live in the
  **5 earned deep blocks**, hero renders riding as asset refs inside them — *not* a block-per-trim or a per-SKU dive quota.
  The pilot-triage lesson held. Had I rostered trims, ~22 rows → 100+; the index-grain rule is what saved it.

**Verdict:** fit — **~85%**, a clean Multi-product seat. The spine carried it; the only real failures were a *lint* artifact
(footnote-glued prices) and *non-unique slugs* on the landing-less commercial chassis. Didn't force it.
