# Consumer Review

Question: **Where did Truffle create reader value, and where did it fall short?**

## Verdict

- **Valuable? Yes** — high-value calibration read.
- **Why:** It closes the generalizability arc with the *first positive non-telehealth
  schema fit* (subscription → transactional-luxury → investor → marketplace), and resolves
  the contracted two-stacked-absence trap in the store's favor: the store is not blind to
  take rate — it holds verbatim fee schedules in prose for 3/5.
- **Where Truffle added value:** (a) a reader can now build marketplace cohort queries on
  `business_model` (the right key) rather than `primary_industry` (which scatters them);
  (b) the reader knows exactly what prose will and won't give them — fee schedules yes,
  a filterable take-rate field no; (c) the `[published]` price-visibility token's grain
  mismatch on two-sided platforms is named (G1).
- **Where Truffle added little or fell short:** G2 (filings source family) and G3
  (business_model-as-cohort-key) are true but well-anticipated from prior runs / scout.md
  — low incremental teaching. G1 stops one step short of the *readability trap* (see new
  observation R1).
- **What the consumer can do now:** assemble + compare the marketplace cohort, read each
  brand's monetization from prose, and know which 2 (airbnb, uber) need a filings panel
  for fee %.
- **What made it safer than generic Claude + web search:** the verbatim, dated,
  source-located fee quotes (etsy /sell, doordash /about FAQ, upwork /pricing/client) and
  the disciplined *not-captured ≠ not-charged* language — generic search would assert a
  fee % for airbnb/uber off a secondary blog.
- **Biggest limit:** n=5, heterogeneous, unit-incomparable numbers — a schema-fit read,
  not a marketplace-market claim.
- **Human follow-up needed:** none required; the bounded-live follow-ups (Scout C4/C5)
  are optional and consumer-gated.
