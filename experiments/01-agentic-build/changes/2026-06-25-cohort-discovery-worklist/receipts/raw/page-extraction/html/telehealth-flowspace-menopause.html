    15 Telehealth Companies That Are Making Online Menopause Treatment More Accessible

    Because menopause care should be available to everyone.
    By Chloe Castleberry, Maggie Ryan
    February 17, 2026 12:43 pm

    More than 1 million women in the United States experience menopause each year. The vast majority of them face a lack of care when it comes to receiving proper treatment for their symptoms, which can range from hot flashes and night sweats to anxiety and mood swings. During this transition, women are also likely to experience changes in cardiovascular and bone health, losing an average of 10 percent of their bone mass in the first five years after menopause, studies say. And when we can’t find the care we need from our local providers, online menopause companies may be a solution.

    Menopause happens to half the population, but it can be a challenging, confusing, and isolating time, especially without the proper resources to guide you through the transition. You might not have access to care due to many factors, from a busy schedule to a local deficiency in specialists; many doctors, in fact, receive almost no training in menopause care. These concerning trends contribute to the longevity paradox: the fact that women, on average, live longer than men but live more years in poorer health. Increasing access to affordable healthcare during menopause — a time in life when women’s health shifts dramatically — can help.

    A different kind of care has stepped in to fill the gap. We’ve seen a boom in telehealth companies offering online menopause treatment over the last few years, in response to the demand for healthcare options that meet women where they are and offer real, effective solutions. And women are ready and willing to invest in their own health when given the opportunity to do so. For proof, just look at Midi Health, one of the leading telehealth companies in this space, which recently reached “unicorn” status by hitting $1 billion valuation. “For decades, women were told their symptoms were normal and untreatable,” Joanna Strober, CEO and cofounder of Midi Health, told Flow Space earlier this month. “What we’re proving is that specialized women’s health care can scale, and when it does, the entire health care system works better.” 

    We did the research and found 15 telehealth companies offering online menopause treatment that women can receive from the comfort of their homes, no matter where they live. (Note that most of the menopause telehealth companies below charge you per visit, with costs for prescriptions paid at the pharmacy, while others charge a monthly or yearly membership fee instead. We’ve also pointed out the companies that accept health insurance.)

    Winona
    check out winona
    Doctor’s visits can be tedious and, for some, not even worth it. Luckily, Winona is changing that by making care accessible for women across the U.S. Winona’s goal is to take the confusion out of menopause and help women get the solutions they need. How? Through education and proper treatment throughout the entirety of a woman’s menopause journey. This platform offers doctor-prescribed, bioidentical hormone replacement therapy that is backed by science and shipped directly to your door. 
    Midi Health
    $250 for initial visit 
    Midi is a virtual care clinic for women 40+, catered to treating perimenopause and menopause symptoms. This includes but is not limited to brain fog, insomnia, joint pain, and weight changes. What makes Midi great is of course the telehealth aspect, but also the fact that it was created by women over 40, making it that much easier to receive care from someone who actually knows what you’re going through. For payment, Midi accepts health insurance, and if you’re not insured, you can book an initial visit fr $250, followed by continued visits at $150. (According to Midi, most patients with insurance pay an average of $50 per visit.)
    Wisp
    $99 for consult 
    Wisp offers telehealth with a focus on reproductive healthcare — they offer treatments for yeast infections, UTIs, bacterial vaginosis, and STIs like herpes. But this company has also expanded into menopause care, offering an array of treatment options from minoxidil for hair growth to estrogen hormone treatment. Wisp even offers products from Stripes Beauty, Naomi Watts’ menopause care company.

    It all starts with a $99 initial consult, which also comes with follow-up visits and three months of access to Wisp’s care team. Wisp doesn’t accept health insurance — the company says this helps it keep costs low and offer faster response times — but if you ask your Wisp healthcare provider to send prescriptions to your local pharmacy, you can use your insurance to cover the medication’s co-pay.
    Alloy
    Check out Alloy
    Founded by Anne Fulenwider and Monica Molenaar — two friends in their 40s who know firsthand what it’s like to have a lack of healthcare solutions — Alloy is built on the idea that women deserve better. Through medical expertise and evidence-based, holistic solutions recommended after you fill out a questionnaire, Alloy promises to deliver on its motto of women deserving better, starting with quality, online care. And if you’re looking for more support, Alloy just partnered with Menopause Mandate to create a menopause advice line where you can schedule a 15-minute video appointment with a “Menopause Guide” — totally free — to talk out your symptoms, ask questions, and get advice for finding a provider.
    PlushCare
    check out plushcare
    PlushCare offers a simple three-step solution to getting treated for menopause: you’ll book an appointment with one of their providers, talk out your symptoms and experiences, and get a prescription that meets your needs. This telehealth service prides itself on being easy and efficient, and judging from the fact that you can book a same-day appointment to treat your anxiety, mood swings, fatigue, and hot flashes, we’d say it delivers. PlushCare also accepts insurance, which isn’t the case for all the options on this list, so be sure to check out the list of insurances that cover their services.
    Evernow
    $35+/month 
    Recommended to us by Flow Advisory Council member Stacy London, Evernow is on a mission to tackle menopause and everything that comes with it. The healthcare service delivers menopause care by prescribing treatment and by taking a responsive approach through listening and learning carried out by practitioners and researchers. To use Evernow, you can choose from three membership tiers of varying prices — the most expensive is the month-to-month plan at $49/month, and the cheapest is the 12-month plan at $35/month. As a bonus, you can use your health insurance to cover the cost of video visits and most medications prescribed through Evernow.
    Allara Health
    $20/month 
    Allara is tailored to your specific hormonal health, assembling a personalized care team and health plan for your needs — whether you have PCOS, endometriosis, an autoimmune condition, are entering perimenopause, or some combination of those hormonal health concerns. You start with some hormonal and metabolic testing, and then meet with an OB/GYN or nurse practitioner. If necessary to meet with a specialist on your particular condition, Allara will link you up with one. Either way, you also meet with a registered dietitian to connect all the dots of diet, stress, sleep, and overall lifestyle in order to care for your hormone health. Allara runs on a membership-based model and accepts health insurance; according to the website, a monthly membership typically comes out to $20/month with insurance.
    Joi + Blokes
    $89+/month 
    Who wouldn’t want to receive healthcare from a company called “Joi”?! Even the name implies that you’ll leave your telehealth appointment feeling well taken care of. You can get complete a la carte menopause care at Joi, which gives you the option of paying $50 a month to get access to hormonal therapy medications that might not be fully covered by health insurance, like testosterone. You can book hormonal testing through the company ($149 for a full panel), stock up on topical meds to improve libido (Joi’s “Scream Cream“), and explore GLP-1’s, as Joi offers a Zepbound subscription.
    Interlude
    Check Out Interlude
    Interlude was created to specifically help women experiencing some of the vaginal and urinary symptoms of menopause such as vaginal dryness, itching, burning, painful sex, frequent UTIs, and urgency to pee. You start by filling out a health profile, which is then reviewed within hours. A prescription for estradiol (either a cream or suppositories) is then delivered straight to your door. (Note: Interlude doesn’t accept health insurance.) The best part? You can still message your doctor for free if you have any medical questions. Interlude will also text before every refill in case you need to cancel, postpone, or make any other changes.
    Stella
    Check out Stella
    The Stella approach focuses on making information easy to understand and accessible across its website and app. The app was created in collaboration with a range of clinicians and healthcare professionals, along with its blog, The Latest. What makes Stella more than a telehealth company is the sense of community the brand has created. Through its app and blog, women can share their menopause stories and learn more about the transition through vetted psychotherapists, nutritionists, personal trainers, sexologists, and more. This platform also accepts health insurance.
    Gennev
    Check out gennev
    Similar to Alloy, Gennev offers a free menopause assessment. The assessment takes about five minutes and by the end, you’ll find out your menopause type and options for support to relieve your symptoms. One of the benefits of Gennev is the adjusted care plan. It’s clear this is a company that understands care is not a linear path. Your body is going through many changes during this period, and if menopause care needs to adapt, Gennev will adjust accordingly. Best of all: Gennev is covered by insurance through Aetna, Anthem, and United Healthcare.
    HerMD Health 
    Check out HerMd Health
    From hot flashes and changes in hormone levels to vaginal dryness and painful sex, the team at HerMD Health is here to treat all of your menopause symptoms and concerns. What makes this telehealth company different is its comprehensive focus on individual symptoms along with an array of services that are often fragmented in an in-person doctor’s office. Additionally, unlike a typical OB-GYN appointment, which could last as little as 15 minutes, HerMD’s appointments are 20-60 minutes and provide gynecological, sexual health, and menopause-focused support for patients. They’re also covered by multiple health insurance companies.
    Pandia Health
    $35+/month 
    Pandia Health offers a range of menopause treatments, from pills and creams to patches, vaginal rings, and vaginal suppositories. How do you know which one is right for you? Their experts will help. You share your symptoms and menopause experience so far, and Pandia Health’s doctors prescribe you a bioidentical hormone treatment plan that fits your needs. Your meds are delivered to you for free, and you’re on your way to relief. Pandia Health also accepts multiple insurance plans to help with payment.
    Tia Health
    check out tia health
    Tia Health provides both in-person and virtual care in six states so far, including a menopause and perimenopause assessment. You’ll book a virtual appointment with a healthcare provider from the company, who will discuss your symptoms and recommend a range of treatments, from lifestyle recommendations like diet and exercise to prescription meds like antidepressants, hormone therapy, and symptom-specific treatment to address things like hot flashes and vaginal dryness. Tia Health also accepts health insurance, including most major PPOs.
    Intimate Rose
    check out intimate rose
    Intimate Rose offers prescriptions for menopause treatments like estradiol gel and patches, progesterone, and vaginal estrogen. If non-hormonal options are your preference, the brand has that too: Paroxetine and Desvenlafaxine are both available to help ease symptoms. You’ll start the process with a free online assessment, which can give you an idea of which treatments are right for you. From there, Intimate Rose provides the option of moving forward with a physician review and personal consultation. Any prescriptions get shipped right to your door.
