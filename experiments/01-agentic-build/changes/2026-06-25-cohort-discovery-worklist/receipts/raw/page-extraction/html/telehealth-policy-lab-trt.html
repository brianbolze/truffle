Fatigue that sleep does not fix. A libido that has quietly disappeared. Brain fog, irritability, and a body that no longer responds to the gym the way it used to. These can be symptoms of low testosterone1, a condition that affects up to 13.8 million men in the United States2. Yet many men never receive a diagnosis or treatment.

Testosterone replacement therapy (TRT) has been a recognized treatment for clinically low testosterone for decades3. What has changed is access. Telehealth platforms now allow patients to consult with licensed clinicians, complete lab work, and receive prescribed medications without visiting a traditional urology or endocrinology office.

But the market is noisy. Dozens of online TRT clinics compete for attention, each promising transparent pricing, expert care, and fast results. The reality is more complicated. Pricing structures vary dramatically, from flat monthly subscriptions to pay-per-service models where the true cost only becomes clear after multiple appointments. Some clinics prescribe FDA-approved testosterone, while others rely on compounded formulations from partner pharmacies. Cancellation policies range from genuinely flexible to effectively non-refundable. And not every provider requires the lab work and ongoing monitoring that safe TRT demands.

Here, we’ll look at five online TRT providers: Male Excel, Peter MD, Hone Health, Fountain TRT, and Defy Medical. We’ll cover verified pricing, medication formats, clinical oversight, cancellation policies, fees that may not be obvious upfront, and real customer feedback. 

The goal is not to declare a winner, but to give you the specific details you need to make an informed decision based on your priorities.

What to Consider When Choosing an Online TRT Clinic

Below are some things to consider when choosing a TRT clinic.

TRT Formats: Injections, Creams, Gels, and More

Online clinics typically offer injectable testosterone cypionate, which is the most common format. Some also offer topical creams, gels, pellets, nasal gels, or oral therapies3. Cream and gel are not the same product. Creams are typically compounded, while gels are more often FDA-approved formulations like AndroGel3,4. Not every clinic offers every format, and availability often depends on what their partner pharmacy compounds.

FDA-Approved vs. Compounded Testosterone

FDA-approved testosterone products have undergone formal regulatory review for safety and efficacy5. Compounded medications are mixed by specialty pharmacies for individual patients and are not FDA-approved. Compounded products are widely used when clinically appropriate, but they are not equivalent to FDA-approved products in terms of regulatory review. Ask your provider which type you are receiving.

Pricing Transparency

Advertised monthly prices rarely tell the whole story. Some clinics bundle labs, consultations, and medication into one fee. Others charge separately for each component. Entry costs, lab processing fees, and medication costs can add $50 to $200 per month on top of a base subscription. Always calculate the true all-in monthly cost before committing.

Clinical Oversight

Any reputable TRT provider requires blood work before prescribing and at regular intervals afterward. Look for clinics that mandate labs at least every six months, offer video consultations with licensed physicians or mid-level providers, and adjust dosing based on lab results rather than symptoms alone.

Cancellation and Refund Policies

Some clinics allow cancellation at any time. Others require multi-month commitments or auto-renew with short notice windows. Shipped medications are also generally non-refundable. Read the fine print before entering payment information.

Red Flags

Here are red flags to look out for with any potential TRT provider:

Before you sign up: Legitimate providers should be transparent from the start. Use caution if a clinic does not clearly disclose pricing before onboarding, does not identify its prescribing clinicians, or makes specific outcome guarantees.

Before your first prescription: A reputable TRT provider should require baseline bloodwork before prescribing testosterone. Baseline testing commonly includes total testosterone, and may include free testosterone, LH, FSH, hematocrit/CBC, PSA when age- or risk-appropriate, and other labs based on the clinician’s judgment. 

Be equally wary of clinics that skip a real clinical consultation. If the process relies only on an asynchronous questionnaire without clear clinician review, that may be a sign to ask more questions before proceeding.

After you’re a patient: Red flags here include no scheduled follow-up labs to monitor hematocrit, estradiol, and PSA (which can be affected by TRT); no discussion of risks or contraindications; and early promotion of add-ons like HCG, peptides, or other compounds before your baseline treatment plan is established.

A trustworthy TRT provider treats ongoing monitoring as a core part of treatment.

Conclusion

Choosing the right best online TRT clinic is less about ranking and more about the fit between the provider and your priorities. The right provider depends on what you prioritize: pricing transparency, clinical depth, medication options, or care model flexibility.

Male Excel stands out for its daily microdosing protocol, BBB A+ accreditation, transparent baseline pricing, and 90-day Excel Advantage Guarantee, though final medication costs depend on the prescribed dose. Peter MD offers predictable subscription pricing and the broadest medication menu in this comparison. Hone Health offers a structured onboarding experience for beginners with at-home labs and BBB Accreditation, but does not fully disclose final medication costs upfront. Fountain TRT offers a bundled, concierge-style approach with text support, though its public review footprint is smaller. Defy Medical offers a more customizable pay-per-service model with broad format options. 

Regardless of which provider you choose, look for these core safeguards: blood work before any prescription, regular lab monitoring (at least every 6 months), video consultations with licensed clinicians, and a clear understanding of your total monthly cost before committing. Testosterone is a controlled substance that requires proper medical oversight. If a clinic skips baseline labs or appears to prescribe testosterone without a thorough evaluation, that is a reason to pause and ask more questions before moving forward.

FAQs

All five providers reviewed here state that they require blood work before prescribing testosterone. This is an important part of responsible TRT care because lab results help clinicians confirm whether testosterone therapy is appropriate and monitor safety over time. If a clinic appears willing to prescribe testosterone without baseline labs or a meaningful clinical evaluation, that is a reason to pause and ask more questions before moving forward.

Costs vary widely depending on the provider, plan, medication, lab fees, and billing model. Among the providers reviewed here, advertised pricing ranges from Peter MD’s $79/month annual plan to Fountain TRT’s $199 per 4 weeks Starter plan. Hone Health’s TRT membership starts at $129/month, but medication is billed separately. Male Excel charges a $99/month membership plus medication costs, while Defy Medical estimates average costs of under $250/month in the first year and under $200/month after that. Always calculate the all-in cost, including labs, memberships, consultations, shipping, and medications.

The five providers reviewed here use licensed clinicians, require lab work, and prescribe medications through partner pharmacies or pharmacy networks. However, the quality, pricing transparency, cancellation policies, and follow-up processes vary by provider. Before enrolling with any online TRT clinic, confirm that it requires blood work, clearly identifies its prescribing clinicians, explains medication sourcing, and provides transparent pricing and cancellation terms.

None of the five providers reviewed here accept insurance for TRT treatment itself. However, some allow HSA, FSA, or HRA funds depending on the provider and the patient’s plan. Defy Medical also allows patients to use their own insurance for lab work through LabCorp, though reimbursement is not guaranteed. Always confirm coverage directly with your insurer or plan administrator.

FDA-approved testosterone products have gone through FDA review for safety, effectiveness, quality, and manufacturing standards. Compounded testosterone is prepared by a compounding pharmacy for an individual patient and is not FDA-approved. Most online TRT clinics reviewed here primarily use compounded medications. Defy Medical is the only provider in this comparison confirmed to offer both compounded options and access to FDA-approved testosterone products.

Cancellation policies vary. Hone Health and Male Excel allow cancellation of the membership, though cancellation generally applies at the end of the current billing period and fees may be non-refundable. Peter MD requires a minimum 6-month commitment on discounted plans and uses auto-renewal with a 48-hour cancellation window. Fountain TRT offers a no-commitment Starter plan, but longer plans require larger upfront payments. Defy Medical does not use a subscription model, so there is no monthly membership to cancel. Shipped medications and completed services are generally non-refundable across these providers.

Be cautious with any clinic that appears to prescribe testosterone without baseline blood work, does not clearly identify its prescribing clinicians, makes guaranteed outcome claims, or does not explain pricing and cancellation terms before payment. It is also worth asking how often follow-up labs are required, which pharmacy dispenses the medication, whether the testosterone is compounded or FDA-approved, and what happens if you need to pause, cancel, or adjust treatment.

All five providers reviewed here offer injectable testosterone. Most also offer topical testosterone cream, which is typically compounded and is different from FDA-approved testosterone gels such as AndroGel. Traditional gel is not confirmed as a standard option for most of the providers reviewed here. Defy Medical appears to offer the broadest range of formats, including injections, topical lotions/gels, pellets, and nasal gel.
